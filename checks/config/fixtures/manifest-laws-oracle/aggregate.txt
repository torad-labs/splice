# LAW [2026-09-18]: MAGNITUDE FROM THE COMP, SIGN FROM HEADROOM (2026-09-18, M1-25; the rule was already in the codebase and nobody had named it). Headroom is not symmetric between the themes. A dark plane sits at the black end with the whole range above it; a light plane sits at the white end with almost nothing above. Measured: paper has 216.7 L below it in dark and 9.2 above it in light; room has 13.4 below in dark and 29.6 above in light. So in dark the world's materials are LIGHTS ON A DARK GROUND, and in light there is no room for them to be lights. Carrying M1-21's deltas across unchanged clips three of twelve, all three highlights: E-1's lip lands at 258.8, E-3's stile highlight at 268.9, E-7's rule divider at 269.6. --bay-slot is the only material in the tree written as an ALPHA over its own plane rather than a hex, and it is the only one that survives inversion: -23.0 L on the light floor, +22.2 L on the dark floor, same magnitude within one level, sign following the theme by itself because an alpha composites against whatever is beneath it. THE LAW: a material's magnitude is measured off the comp and its sign comes from the headroom of the plane it sits on. Write it as an alpha or a color-mix against its own ground. NEVER a hex, which fixes the sign, and NEVER a filter, which fixes the direction -- filter: brightness() multiplies luminance, so it always moves toward black, which recedes on dark and ADVANCES on light. The ghost strip proves it: dark order bay 9.6, room 13.4, ghost 108.7, paper 216.7 -- a faded strip behind the page; light order GHOST 122.9, bay 174.6, room 225.4, paper 245.8 -- the heaviest object on the page. COROLLARY WITH TEETH: when a material must keep its sign in both themes for the object to read, and the light plane has no headroom for it, THE PLANE IS WRONG, NOT THE MATERIAL.

# LAW [2026-09-18]: AN ASSERTION THAT NAMES A DEFECT DIES THE DAY THE DEFECT IS FIXED. Pin the PROPERTY, never the FINDING. Three instances in one night, all from the same correct instinct aimed one sentence off: accounts.test.ts pinned the SET of dispositions instead of asserting every entry has one, and went vacuous when the set emptied; M1-37's verify greped the line wire-check prints only when routes are unserved, and went false the hour those routes were disposed; and a test pinning the absence of three page directories would go red the day someone legitimately adds one back. The tell is grammatical: an assertion whose subject is a number you just measured, or a line you just read in today's output, is pinned to today. An assertion whose subject is a relationship that must hold for the code to be correct survives its own success. THE SAME MECHANISM RUNS ON LEGITIMATE CHANGE TOO, AND IT IS HOW GUARDS ACTUALLY DIE (claude-splice-main, 2026-09-18, improving on this seat's remedy for the V4-157 golden): GUARD THE SHAPE, NEVER THE BYTE. Assert the PROPERTY that must hold (blank text, signature present) and never the VALUE that happens to hold it. A guard is only as durable as its false-positive rate, which is a fact about the people maintaining it and not about the code.
# LAW [2026-09-18]: THE COMP HAS TWO RULE SYSTEMS AND THEY OBEY OPPOSITE RULES. Never quote one ruling at the other.
