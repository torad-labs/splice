# splice — proxy landscape audit and improvement plan

2026-07-26. This is the second full survey of splice's reliability surface, and it is built on top of
the first: `dev/research/gateway-best-practices-2026-07-18.md` found 27 gaps (G1..G28) and
`dev/research/gateway-gaps-tracker.md` records nearly all of them landed. Nothing marked `done` there
is re-litigated here. What follows is the *outside* view: 94 candidate projects swept across five
blind angles (general AI gateways, Claude-Code routers, OAuth-subscription-reuse tools, resilience
infrastructure, just-works UX), 12 deep-dived with their GitHub issue trackers mined for the failure
modes their users actually hit, and 7 splice subsystems inventoried from source. 85 candidates were
generated by four thematic synthesizers and put through adversarial verification against the real
tree: 85 survived, 0 were killed outright, 7 required a corrected premise (their corrections are the
text used below, not the originals). One survivor is a decision surface rather than a proposal
(§9); one was merged into its twin. 83 actionable items follow, each with `file:line` evidence, a
concrete proposal, and the gate that proves it landed.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The highest-leverage moves, one line each:

1. **NF-01 — clamp the 429 cooldown horizon and make `head restart` clear it.** A single
   `Retry-After: 86400` bricks a head until daemon restart, and `HeadServer.restart()` provably
   cannot clear it. **S**.
2. **NF-06 — lift the runaway-buffer guard from the chat dialect into `:spi` and wire it into
   Responses + Passthrough.** One never-terminating upstream can OOM the single JVM and take every
   head down at once. **M**.
3. **JW-01 — stop discarding daemon boot stderr.** `TopologyLoader.loadOrMaterialize` runs *before*
   `persistentLogger` exists and both cold-start paths redirect to `/dev/null`; a first-run failure
   is unreadable by design. **S**.
4. **SH-01/SH-02/SH-04 — one missing-expiry policy, one ineffective-refresh guard, one supervised
   probe loop.** Three providers currently have three accidental behaviours for a token with no
   decodable expiry, grok can burn rotating refresh tokens in an unbounded loop, and the probe that
   is supposed to self-heal cannot restart itself. **S/M/S**.
5. **CX-19 — add a response-side golden per dialect.** Request bytes are contract-pinned; the
   Anthropic SSE splice *emits* is not, so every correctness fix in §8 lands without a regression
   net. Build this first. **M**.
6. **CX-01 — validate tool-call argument JSON before declaring a turn Success.** Truncated arguments
   currently ship as a well-formed-looking `tool_use` with corrupt `input`. **M**.
7. **CX-02 — the COMPACT MODE directive exists only in the Responses dialect.** On kimi and every
   openai-chat head a compaction turn is an ordinary tool-stripped turn, and a chatty non-summary is
   stored *silently* as the session's summary. **S**.
8. **JW-02/JW-05 — make `splice doctor` see runtime.** It prints `Everything checks out.` while every
   head is dead: `/health` already carries `failedHeads` and the CLI throws it away. **S/M**.
9. **NF-02 — reconcile the `maxInflight` default with splice's own measurement.** The shipped default
   is 100; the committed perf note records 67% turn failure at exactly that value. **S**.
10. **NF-03 — enforce the whole-turn wall clock outside an open stream.** `totalCap` is only sampled
    while a stream is live, so an N-round fold gets N × the retry budget against one cap while
    holding its admission slot. **M**.
11. **CX-15 — reject context overflow before dispatch and break the compaction retry deadlock.**
    Depends on CX-03 (the token estimator is bytes/3 today and would reject every image turn). **M**.
12. **JW-03 — one head-tag convention.** Auth-probe, refresh, and boot lines don't contain
    `[<headKey>]`, so the per-head log tail is blind at exactly the moments a head is failing. **M**.

**Where splice already leads the field.** The 2026-07-18 stabilization is not marketing: splice's
transport-exception taxonomy, WHATWG-derived SSE assembly, cross-process credential FileLock,
single-flight refresh with cancellation isolation, merge-not-overwrite atomic 0600 persistence,
commitment rule (never retry after handoff), two-tier watchdog, and typed terminal-precedence are
each *stricter* than the corresponding code in codex, gemini-cli, aider, or LiteLLM. Concretely:
gemini-cli **#21691** (refresh drops the refresh_token) is a bug splice's `mergedAuthJson` structurally
cannot have on grok/codex; kimi-cli's own TOCTOU fix (PR **#1996**) arrives at the same shape splice
landed as G1/G15; aider and claude-code-proxy have *no* retry budget, jitter, or Retry-After handling
at all; 1rgs/claude-code-proxy — the most-starred Anthropic↔OpenAI translator — has zero retry code,
no prompt caching, and drops the `thinking` field for every non-Anthropic backend. splice's error
boundary (exactly one terminal frame per turn, perf row per failure class) has no peer in the pool.
The L1–L4 error-discipline ladder, up to a FIR compiler plugin that makes an unconsumed `Result` a
compile error, is unmatched anywhere in the 94 projects swept.

**Where splice is genuinely behind.** Four places, all structural rather than incidental. (a)
*Bounded accumulation*: the chat dialect has a runaway guard, the other two do not, and splice is one
process serving every head — CLIProxyAPI **#1287/#3947** (50MB→870MB RSS over hours) and litellm
**#25219** are the same daemon shape. (b) *Diagnosability of its own boot and runtime*: doctor is a
static-config checker, boot stderr goes to `/dev/null`, the per-head log filter misses the auth lines,
`/api/perf` has zero consumers, and the per-head override layer that landed on 2026-07-25 is invisible
in `/api/config`. codex ships `codex doctor` with bounded reachability probes; splice's never touches
an upstream. (c) *Dialect symmetry*: the honesty gates, compaction instructions, omission markers, and
usage aliases are each present in one or two of three dialects and absent from the rest — the exact
"guard exists on one path only" class that produced litellm **#33686** and hermes **#70216**. (d)
*Response-side contract*: request bytes are golden-pinned per dialect; the emitted Anthropic SSE has
no equivalent, which is the missing net under everything in §8.

### What each category has converged on

**Claude-Code routers** (opencode, the ~15 `claude-code-proxy`/router forks, claude-code-mux,
Router-Maestro, hysteam/claude-code-router). Convergence: tier-name model mapping
(`BIG/MIDDLE/SMALL` or `opus/sonnet/haiku`), a `/v1/models` endpoint so Claude Code's `/model` picker
works, per-task routing (background / reasoning / long-context / websearch), subagent-aware routing via
a system-prompt tag, and — nearly universally — a fallback chain. splice matches all of it except the
fallback chain, which is a locked non-goal (G27), and the `/v1/models` picker surface, which it does
have. What the category has *not* converged on, and where splice is alone: a reasoning-summary mirror
that survives compaction. 1rgs/claude-code-proxy #9 (`thinking` field silently dropped for every
non-Anthropic backend) is still open; kimi-cli #1864/#1865 shows what happens when the reasoning trace
is a render-time toggle instead of transcript text — a point release deleted it and the users revolted.

**OAuth-subscription reuse** (codex, kimi-cli, gemini-cli, qwen-code, copilot-api, grok-oauth-proxy,
claude-auth-proxy, Hermes). Convergence: proactive refresh ahead of expiry (not reactive on 401);
single-flight/in-flight-refresh dedup; cross-process file locking on the shared credential file; merge
rather than rewrite on persist; a terminal state for `invalid_grant` distinct from a transient failure.
splice matches every one of these except in three specific holes this audit found (SH-01 missing-expiry
policy on codex/kimi, SH-02 ineffective-refresh loop on grok, SH-10 kimi's from-scratch rewrite). Where
the category is *ahead* of splice: typed refresh-failure taxonomy (codex `classify_refresh_token_failure`,
Hermes' `_TERMINAL_AUTH_REASONS` frozenset) and server-side verification of a locally-valid token
(gemini-cli's `getTokenInfo`), which is the un-landed half of splice's own G8.

**General gateways** (LiteLLM, Portkey, Bifrost, Kong, Helicone, Truefoundry, Cloudflare/Vercel AI
Gateway, LangDB). Convergence: per-exception-class retry policy, cooldown/circuit-breaking keyed on
error class rather than blanket status, a *pre-dispatch* budget or quota check, and percentile latency
observability as baseline (p50/p95, TTFT, ITL). Most of the rest of this category — virtual keys, team
budgets, RBAC, Redis-backed cross-pod state, semantic caching — is exactly what splice's single-user
loopback lock rejects, and correctly so. The transferable core is narrow and named in §5/§6: NF-14
(per-class retry allowance), NF-05/G22 (aggregate budget), CX-15 (pre-dispatch overflow check),
JW-12 (surface the perf aggregation splice already computes).

**Resilience infra** (Envoy, HAProxy, nginx, Caddy, Traefik, Linkerd, resilience4j, Polly, Failsafe,
gRPC A6, AWS adaptive retry, Netflix concurrency-limits, systemd, Erlang/OTP). Convergence on five
rules splice should be measured against: (1) bound every ejection/cooldown (`max_ejection_time`); (2)
half-open probe before full re-admission (`permittedNumberOfCallsInHalfOpenState`); (3) a total-time
budget that wraps the retry loop, never sits inside it (Polly's composition rule, Caddy's
`lb_try_duration`); (4) a retry budget as a fraction of live traffic, not a per-request count (Envoy
`RetryBudget`, AWS's 500-token bucket); (5) restart with an intensity limit so a hard-failing child
fails loudly instead of crash-looping (systemd `StartLimitBurst`, OTP's max-restarts-in-period).
splice satisfies (3) partially (NF-03 closes it) and none of (1), (2), (4), (5) — those are NF-01,
NF-15, NF-05, and SH-04/SH-18 respectively. This is the category splice is furthest behind on, and
also the cheapest to close, because every one of these is a bounded change inside `UpstreamClient.kt`
or `AuthProbeLoop.kt`.

## 3. What their issue trackers say will break

The empirical heart of this document. Every class below is a bug someone else already shipped, with
real issue references. For each: splice's exposure, and the file that owns it.

### C1 — Unbounded retry / cooldown loops
`anomalyco/opencode#17648` (~700 API calls in 5s on Azure 429 with `Retry-After: 0` — the
headers-present branch had no cap while the headers-absent branch did), `#21960`, `#33728`;
`Aider-AI/aider#4241`, `#1842` (runaway retry cost, one report $13 in a loop);
`NousResearch/hermes-agent#70401` (credential rotation with no cap on failed-hint rotations became a
non-interruptible loop only an external kill stopped); `envoyproxy/ai-gateway#1866`.
**splice exposure: partially exposed.** The per-attempt loop is bounded correctly (G4a–d), but there
is no aggregate budget across concurrent turns for non-429 classes — that is G22, still `todo` — and
the cooldown horizon itself is unbounded (NF-01). Owner:
`gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/UpstreamClient.kt`.

### C2 — Stream idle vs total timeout misfires
`QwenLM/qwen-code#5975`, `#4604`, `#4711` (a fixed 120s inter-chunk idle timer killing a turn while
the provider was demonstrably still generating; workaround is an env var users discover themselves);
`anomalyco/opencode#28957`, `#31456`, `#33714`; `QwenLM/qwen-code#741` (an opaque 483s wall that was
really retries × per-attempt timeout, with no knob describing the real budget).
**splice exposure: partially exposed.** The two-tier watchdog (firstByte vs streamIdle) is the correct
shape and better than qwen-code's single timer. The residual holes are that `totalCap` is not sampled
outside an open stream (NF-03), the tiers are `restartRequired` knobs so an operator cannot retune
mid-incident (NF-13), and a stall discards the round entirely rather than re-anchoring (NF-11). Owner:
`gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/Watchdog.kt`,
`gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/head/TurnDriver.kt:462`.

### C3 — Unbounded accumulation → OOM in a long-lived daemon
`BerriAI/litellm#25219` (steady RSS growth under normal load until OOM-kill);
`router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI#1287`, `#3947`, `#4110` (RSS 50MB→870MB over hours, CPU pegged, recovery
only via restart); `google-gemini/gemini-cli#19590` (fatal V8 heap crash from in-memory transcript
duplication during compaction); `anomalyco/opencode#30086`, `#29177`.
**splice exposure: fully exposed on two of three dialects.** The guard exists only in
`gateway/dialect-openai-chat/.../ChatStreamTranslator.kt:353-368`. `ResponsesStreamTranslator.kt:316`
and `PassthroughStreamTranslator.kt:54` accumulate into structurally identical unbounded
`StringBuilder`s. Because spliced is one process serving every head, that OOM is a total outage, not a
turn failure — and there is no supervisor to restart it (SH-18). NF-06 closes it.

### C4 — SSE parse, chunk-boundary, and terminal-frame bugs
`BerriAI/litellm#25429`, `#25403`, `#26219` (Responses stream ends with `output: []` even though a
prior `output_item.done` carried the content — users independently wrote the same accumulator patch);
`#25214`, `#25216`, `#27492` (first chunk after a reasoning→text block transition silently dropped —
named a blocker for Claude Code); `envoyproxy/ai-gateway#2162`, `#2391` (a terminal usage event split
across two body reads is lost because each chunk is parsed independently); `QwenLM/qwen-code#7649`
(empty-`choices` usage-carrier frame classified as end-of-stream, zeroing token accounting).
**splice exposure: largely immune, with one named residual.** G12 landed WHATWG-derived event
assembly and G9 landed malformed-frame telemetry, both table-driven-tested in
`gateway/provider-spi/src/test/kotlin/SseReaderTest.kt` — this is the strongest-pinned file in the
tree and it structurally rules out the chunk-boundary class. The residual is the *emitted* direction:
nothing byte-diffs the Anthropic SSE splice writes, so a reordering or dropped frame class on the
output side has no gate (CX-19). Owner: `gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/wire/SseEmitter.kt`.

### C5 — Tool-call argument truncation and `tool_use`/`tool_result` pairing
`anomalyco/opencode#36766` (Responses path finalizes tool calls with truncated JSON arguments ending
mid-string, treated as terminal), `#38747`, `#34652` (double-JSON-encoded args);
`BerriAI/litellm#32607` (per-chunk `id` churn broke downstream argument aggregation);
`anomalyco/opencode#25774`, `#5750`, `#31048` (message normalization *and* compaction independently
produce an assistant `tool_use` with no following `tool_result`; "Anthropic rejects the whole request",
"permanently wedging the session"); `QwenLM/qwen-code#656`, `#528`.
**splice exposure: fully exposed on both halves.** Neither translator ever parses the accumulated
argument buffer (`ChatStreamTranslator.kt:272`, `ResponsesStreamTranslator.kt:539-559`) — CX-01. And
`appendToolResult` (`ResponsesRequestBuilder.kt:635-659`) emits `function_call_output` unconditionally
with no check that the matching `function_call` was ever emitted — CX-10. The repo already carries
orphan-awareness for the *reasoning* item family (`ToolSurfaceRecovery.kt`, `ReasoningCache.kt:139-153`),
which proves the failure class is understood and the tool-pairing half was simply never built.

### C6 — Silent degradation: dropped fields, empty responses recorded as success
`portkey-ai/gateway#1672` (a Responses request silently falls back to the chatComplete path, dropping
reasoning/instructions/truncation/previous_response_id, with a plausible 200), `#1217`, `#1423`
(`temperature=0` dropped by a falsy check); `envoyproxy/ai-gateway#2206` (Anthropic system messages
silently dropped translating to Bedrock), `#1974`, `#2318`; `anomalyco/opencode#37372`, `#30411`,
`#37735` (reasoning-only responses recorded as successful assistant turns — "makes long GUI sessions
appear stuck"); `google-gemini/gemini-cli#28351` (200 + `STOP` + zero-length text is indistinguishable
from success; "the number one complaint on my company's gemini slack channel").
**splice exposure: partially exposed, and the honesty gate is real.** `TurnPipeline.kt:64-83` already
converts an empty terminal into an honest `api_error` and promotes reasoning to text above a floor —
this is why splice does *not* have opencode's silent-empty-turn bug. The residual exposures are all
about *which* fields vanish without a counter: `UnknownBlock`/passthrough `document` (CX-04),
`stop_sequences`/`temperature`/`top_p` on the chat dialect (CX-05),
`disable_parallel_tool_use` (CX-06), the chat `refusal` field (CX-08), passthrough's missing
content-filter branch (CX-07), and a message that maps to zero chat messages (CX-20).

### C7 — OAuth refresh lifecycle
`google-gemini/gemini-cli#21691` (refresh drops the refresh_token, sessions die ~1h later),
`#21956` (silent refresh failure hangs with zero output), `#28341` (a second process sharing the
credential file deletes it mid-flight → infinite re-login loop), `#26715` (corrupted credential file
crash-loops instead of degrading); `anomalyco/opencode#37059`, `#34074` (cross-process refresh-token
rotation → `invalid_grant` for the peer); `openai/codex#14144`, `#29630` (re-auth succeeds on disk but
the running process keeps the stale in-memory token); `MoonshotAI/kimi-cli` PR **#1996** (the TOCTOU
fix: never delete on a single 401, tombstone instead); `router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI#3654`, `#1015`,
`#4120` (provider-side revocation indistinguishable from a proxy bug).
**splice exposure: mostly immune, three named holes.** G1 (FileLock + re-read-before-refresh +
peer-rotation adoption), G15 (mtime-keyed `invalid_grant` latch), and merge-not-overwrite persistence
close the majority of this class better than any surveyed project. The holes: codex/kimi treat a
missing expiry three different ways (SH-01), grok can loop on a *successful but ineffective* refresh
(SH-02), kimi rewrites its shared credential file from scratch (SH-10), and no non-`invalid_grant`
terminal rejection has any cooldown memory (SH-05).

### C8 — Context overflow and compaction deadlock
`openai/codex#24014`, `#23589`, `#22890`, `#33008`, `#24418` (auto-compaction of an already-oversized
context fails with `context_length_exceeded`; every retry re-sends the same payload; the thread is
"effectively dead" and the only workaround is abandoning it); `BerriAI/litellm#33686` (context-window
pre-call checks enforced on chat/completions and *silently skipped* on the Responses path);
`anomalyco/opencode#25774`/`#31048` (compaction slicing history on a token boundary and orphaning a
`tool_use`); `MoonshotAI/kimi-cli#2496` (a compaction marker leaking raw into the resumed transcript);
`MoonshotAI/kimi-cli#2011` (client context accounting under 50% while the provider rejects for
exceeding the limit).
**splice exposure: fully exposed and it is the most consequential class here.** `prepareTurn`
(`HeadServer.kt:352-370`) validates model membership and nothing else; overflow is discovered only as
an upstream 400 after quota is spent. `TurnPipeline.kt:75-80` then emits
`"compact returned no content from model — retry"` for a deterministically failing payload — the exact
codex deadlock. And the COMPACT MODE instruction block only exists in the Responses dialect (CX-02),
so on kimi and any openai-chat head the compaction turn is never even *told* to summarize. CX-15,
CX-02, CX-03 in that dependency order.

### C9 — Version skew between cooperating components
`1rgs/claude-code-proxy#12`, `#35` ("doesn't seem to work with the latest Claude Code 0.2.69" — a
proxy written against an older Messages shape, both issues still open with duplicate confused users);
`openai/codex#29140` (a VS Code extension injects a feature flag the paired binary doesn't know →
app-server crash, fixed several releases later); `envoyproxy/ai-gateway#2346`, `#2091` (controller
expects config apiVersion `v1.0.0`, reads `dev`, fails outright post-upgrade);
`MoonshotAI/kimi-cli#2496`.
**splice exposure: partially exposed.** G10's shim version marker plus the `/health` version handshake
cover the splice↔splice axis well. The uncovered axis is splice↔Claude Code: compaction detection is
pinned to five verbatim sentences traced out of Claude Code **2.1.207**
(`gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/compact/Compact.kt:32-39`) with a shadow classifier
that records drift and *nothing that reads it* — `tail()` has zero callers in main sources. JW-07 and
CX-12.

### C10 — Config invisibility and drift
`router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI#4433` (a mutate-on-first-run secret rewrite produced 401s that "look like a
proxy bug"; closed only after proving which config path the process had loaded);
`QwenLM/qwen-code#1015`, `#1210` (diffuse "which layer won" confusion, answered upstream by publishing
a six-layer precedence table); `envoyproxy/ai-gateway#2230` (cross-namespace refs silently pinned to
the wrong namespace); `Aider-AI/aider#3618`, `#4583` ("model database ain't right" — static metadata
drifting from what the provider enforces).
**splice exposure: fully exposed.** Topology is read once at boot (`Main.kt:47-48`); nothing compares
the file on disk to what the daemon booted with (JW-04); the per-head override layer that landed in
`8a1f431` is invisible in `/api/config` because `ConfigLayers` has no per-head field
(`ConfigService.kt:377-383`, JW-06); there is no CLI answer to "which layer set this knob" (JW-09);
and two heads on the same port surface as a generic `Address already in use` rather than a topology
collision (JW-13).

### C11 — Process lifecycle: unsupervised daemons, cleanup races, resource leaks
`NousResearch/hermes-agent#70344` (after a plain network blip the process stayed "running" and never
reconnected for ~22h; the escalation branch never re-armed); `BerriAI/litellm#32750` (a `TypeError`
during streaming-disconnect cleanup), `#26192` (a credential refresh blocking the event loop for 30s
and tripping liveness probes); `google-gemini/gemini-cli#28265`, `#28280`;
`anomalyco/opencode#32175`, `#37626`.
**splice exposure: partially exposed.** The shutdown path is genuinely strong (bounded parallel head
stop with a JVM-halt watchdog floor, pinned by `DaemonStopDeadlineTest`). The exposures are on the
*self-healing* axis: `AuthProbeLoop`'s launched coroutine dies permanently on any RuntimeException
outside `runCatchingCancellable`'s three-type catch list and nothing restarts it (SH-04); there is no
OS-level supervision at all (SH-18); and `persistentLogger`'s in-memory byte counter is never
reconciled after a failed rotate, which can wedge daemon.log silently forever (SH-14).

### C12 — Quota and rate-limit opacity
`openai/codex#28879` (effective per-token cost against the plan window jumped 10–20×, draining a 5h
budget in 2–3 prompts, no client-side signal), `#9508`, `#5999` (47/43 comments on making the weekly
reset deterministic); `router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI#4348` (asking for 429 + `Retry-After` instead of a
bare timeout); `sst/opencode#15988` (visible countdown + "Retry Now"); `MoonshotAI/kimi-cli#2077`
(429 `engine_overloaded` under a paid plan, traced to gateway UA filtering).
**splice exposure: partially exposed.** splice already extracts and humanizes quota resets
(`UpstreamFailureClassifier.kt:100-108` `quotaSuffix`) — ahead of most of the pool. But the cooldown
horizon it computes from them is invisible everywhere: no `/api/heads` field, no dashboard pill, no

## 5. Improvements — never fails

### NF-01 — Unbounded, unclearable per-head 429 cooldown horizon

**Problem** A single 429 whose `Retry-After` says a large number arms a head-wide fail-fast horizon of
exactly that length: no ceiling, no half-open probe, no clear-on-recovery, no operator escape short of
killing the daemon. ChatGPT quota errors legitimately carry multi-day resets — splice's own classifier
humanizes `resets_in_seconds` as "6d 4h" and the code comment cites a real 142h reset observed
2026-07-26. `accumulateAndGet(max)` means the *longest value ever seen* wins permanently, so one
malformed pushback poisons the head for every concurrent and future turn.

**Evidence** CLIProxyAPI caps rate-limit backoff at `quotaBackoffMax = 30m` regardless of provider
pushback (`sdk/cliproxy/auth/conductor_cooldown.go`, `conductor_refresh.go`) and ships
`max-retry-interval` (`config.example.yaml:144-152`) precisely to bound how long a cooling credential
may block. Envoy bounds ejection with
[`max_ejection_time`](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/api-v3/config/cluster/v3/outlier_detection.proto).
LiteLLM's cooldown handler re-admits after a bounded window rather than a provider-declared one
(`litellm/router_utils/cooldown_handlers.py`).

**splice today** `gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/UpstreamClient.kt:406-412` —
`val until = clock() + (failed.retryAfterMs ?: DEFAULT_RATE_LIMIT_COOLDOWN_MS)` then
`rateLimitedUntilMs.accumulateAndGet(until) { c, cand -> maxOf(c, cand) }`, with `retryAfterMs`
(`:361-362`) an uncapped `seconds * 1000`. The `RETRY_AFTER_GIVE_UP_MS` clamp at `:418-422` is
**unreachable for 429s** — the `status == RATE_LIMITED` branch returns GIVE_UP at `:412` first, so G3's
absurd-pushback guard never touches the cooldown horizon. Fail-fast at `:295-304`. The `AtomicLong`
lives on the `UpstreamClient` built once at `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/Daemon.kt:672-677`;
`HeadServer.restart()` (`gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/head/HeadServer.kt:127-141`)
rebuilds the Netty engine and calls `driver.resetHealth()` only, so `POST /api/heads/{key}/restart`
cannot clear it.

**Proposal** Three bounded changes in `UpstreamClient.kt`: (1) clamp the armed horizon to a new
`MAX_RATE_LIMIT_COOLDOWN_MS` (suggest `120_000L` — long enough to starve a herd, short enough that a
recovering account resumes inside one client-retry cycle) while keeping the *full* pushback value in
the surfaced message so the operator still learns the real reset; (2) add
`internal fun clearRateLimitCooldown()` and call it from `HeadServer.startLocked()` next to
`driver.resetHealth()`, making restart a real escape hatch; (3) expose `rateLimitedForMs` on the
snapshot so NF-10/JW-11 can surface it. Leave `RETRY_AFTER_GIVE_UP_MS` untouched.

**Effort** S. **Risk** A genuinely hour-long provider limit gets re-probed every 2 minutes instead of
once — bounded, ~30 extra single requests per hour per head, and NF-15's half-open probe reduces it to
one probe per window. `clearRateLimitCooldown` widens HeadServer's surface onto UpstreamClient by one
read/write accessor.

**Verification** `UpstreamClientRetryPolicyTest.kt:169` currently *pins* honour-the-full-pushback with
a 30s case — that test must be rewritten, not extended. New cases: a 429 with `Retry-After: 86400` arms
a horizon no longer than `MAX_RATE_LIMIT_COOLDOWN_MS` (assert via injected clock plus a follow-up post
that succeeds once the clamp expires); the surfaced message still contains the true pushback. New case
in `gateway/gateway/src/test/kotlin/head/HeadServerCapacityTest.kt`: `restart()` clears an armed
cooldown. Gate: `npm run gate`.

### NF-02 — `maxInflight` default is 100, the value splice measured as 67% turn failure

**Problem** The shipped admission ceiling is the concurrency level splice itself measured as producing
catastrophic turn failure. Any head without an explicit override runs there. A workflow fan-out bursts
100 concurrent streams into one upstream account, gets 429'd, arms the head-wide cooldown (NF-01), and
every remaining turn fails fast.

**Evidence** Netflix concurrency-limits exists because a static ceiling above real capacity converts
load into failure ([VegasLimit/Gradient2Limit](https://github.com/Netflix/concurrency-limits)); Envoy's
`max_requests`/`max_pending_requests` are documented as the mechanism that keeps a saturating upstream
from being driven past capacity. `anomalyco/opencode#17648` and `CLIProxyAPI#4536` are both the same
shape: unbounded concurrency plus retries into a rate-limited account multiplying one limit into an
outage.

**splice today** `gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/config/Knob.kt:139` —
`MAX_INFLIGHT("maxInflight", KnobKind.NUMBER, listOf("CLAUDEX_MAX_INFLIGHT"), 100L)`. The contradicting
measurement is committed at `config/splice.example.toml:202` ("0.3% turn failure at inflight<=14, 11% at
38, 67% at 100") and repeated at `Daemon.kt:683-684`. Only `[heads.claude-kimi.overrides] maxInflight
= "8"` fixes it in the example.

**Proposal** This is *not* "splice lacks per-head admission control" — commit `8a1f431` (2026-07-25)
built the per-head override layer as the deliberate response to this measurement, reasoning at
`Daemon.kt:680-684` that "the ceiling belongs to the upstream ACCOUNT, not the gateway". The 100
default predates the measurement (`ca16f0f`, 2026-07-23, rationale "leaves headroom for multi-agent
fleets"). The gap is a stale default left unreconciled with a newer measurement now that the override
layer exists to carry exceptions. Change the `MAX_INFLIGHT` default to `12L` (inside the measured
0.3%-failure band, headroom over kimi's 8). Keep `0 = unlimited` as the explicit opt-out; the knob is
already hot (live lambda at `Daemon.kt:686-690`) so no restart-flag change. Update the `Knob.kt:136-139`
comment to cite the perf-JSONL measurement and fix the example TOML narrative so 100 no longer reads as
a sane baseline. **The counter-position deserves naming:** an operator may hold that a global default is
the wrong instrument and every head should carry an explicit override. Against that — a head with no
override is the *common* case (four of five example heads), and shipping a safe default plus overrides
for genuinely fast backends fails safe rather than failing at 67%.

**Effort** S. **Risk** A high-capacity backend (local vLLM/Ollama, an enterprise API-key account) now
queues instead of admitting — added latency under fan-out, not failure, because the gate is FIFO-queueing
until `maxQueued=512`. One TOML line restores old behaviour. Behaviour change to a shipped default:
CHANGELOG entry required.

**Verification** `gateway/core/src/test/kotlin/ConfigServiceTest.kt`: assert the new effective default
and that `0`/unlimited still resolves to 0. `HeadServerCapacityTest.kt`: a head with no override admits
at the new ceiling and queues beyond it. `checks/e2e/heads-e2e.sh` tier-1 still green. Gate:
`npm run gate`.

### NF-06 — Responses and Passthrough translators have no runaway-buffer guard

**Problem** `ChatStreamTranslator` has an explicit bounded-memory safety valve that trips into an honest
failure above 20M accumulated chars. The Responses and Passthrough translators accumulate into
structurally identical unbounded `StringBuilder`s with no equivalent cap. A misbehaving or hostile
upstream streaming deltas indefinitely without ever sending `response.completed`/`message_stop` grows
them until the ~1G-capped JVM heap dies. Because spliced is **one process serving every head**, that OOM
is not a turn failure — it is a simultaneous total outage of codex, grok, kimi and every other head, with
no supervisor to restart it (there is no systemd unit; recovery waits for the next wrapper invocation).

**Evidence** `BerriAI/litellm#25219` (unbounded process memory growth under normal load until OOM-kill
in a long-lived proxy); `router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI#1287`/`#3947`/`#4110` (RSS 50MB→870MB over hours, CPU
pegged, full recovery only after restart); `google-gemini/gemini-cli#19590` (fatal V8 heap crash from
in-memory transcript duplication takes down the whole process). All three are the same class.

**splice today** Guard present only in
`gateway/dialect-openai-chat/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/chat/ChatStreamTranslator.kt` — the
`runawayGuard` latch field at `:56`, the per-event check at `:88-90`
(`bufferOverCapacity(textBuf.length, thinkingBuf.length, toolCount, pendingArgsChars)`), the constants
at `:353-355` (`MAX_BUFFERED_CHARS = 20_000_000`), the predicate at `:360-368`. Absent in
`gateway/dialect-openai-responses/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/responses/ResponsesStreamTranslator.kt:316-317`
(`var thinkingBuf/textBuf = StringBuilder()`) and `:327` (`val reasoningEnvelopes = mutableListOf<String>()`),
and in `gateway/dialect-anthropic-passthrough/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/passthrough/PassthroughStreamTranslator.kt:54-55`.
Per-frame caps exist (`SseReader.kt:293-294`, 1MB line / 4MB event) but bound one frame, not the sum.
`Watchdog`'s `totalCap` bounds turn *wall time*, which caps memory only indirectly at (delta rate × cap).

**Proposal** Lift `MAX_BUFFERED_CHARS`/`bufferOverCapacity` into a shared `splice.spi.BufferCapacity`
helper — one definition, three call sites, the same single-source move `TerminalStates.kt` already made
for terminal precedence. Wire into `ResponsesStreamTranslator.driveTurn`'s collect (checking
`thinkingBuf.length`, `textBuf.length`, `reasoningEnvelopes.sumOf { it.length }`) and into
Passthrough's collect. On trip, set a local `runawayGuard` message and stop consuming, exactly as Chat
does — a clean `TurnOutcome.Failure(API_ERROR, ..., providerReported = false)`, never a crash, never a
provider attribution.

**Effort** M. **Risk** A legitimately enormous single response (20M chars) is failed rather than
delivered; that ceiling has shipped in the Chat dialect without incident. Touches three dialect modules;
keep Chat byte-identical so its existing tests are the extraction's regression oracle.

**Verification** New tests mirroring the Chat guard in the Responses and Passthrough test suites: a
synthetic upstream emitting deltas past the cap terminates with a non-provider-reported `API_ERROR` and
bounded memory, not a hang or OOM. Existing `ChatStreamTranslatorTest` cases must stay green unchanged
(proves pure code motion). Gate: `npm run gate` plus the `.rules/` ast-grep walls.

### CX-15 — No pre-dispatch context-overflow check, and an empty compaction is told to "retry" forever

**Problem** splice knows each head's context window and knows the request it is about to send, and never
compares them. Overflow is discovered only as an upstream rejection, after quota is spent. Worse: when
the *compaction* turn is what overflows (or otherwise returns nothing), the pipeline emits
`api_error: "compact returned no content from model — retry"`, and Claude Code retries the identical,
deterministically-failing payload. That is the exact codex deadlock — a thread that can neither proceed
nor compact, recoverable only by abandoning the session.

**Evidence** `openai/codex#24014` ("Codex app automatic context compaction fails with
context_length_exceeded"), `#23589`, `#22890`, `#33008`, `#24418` — every retry re-sends the same
oversized payload, the thread is "effectively dead", the only workaround is manual archiving.
`BerriAI/litellm#33686` is the sibling: context-window pre-call checks enforced on chat/completions and
silently skipped on the Responses path.

**splice today** `gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/head/HeadServer.kt:352-370`
(`prepareTurn`) validates only `provider.catalog.contains(parsed.typed.model)` — no size comparison —
even though `ModelCatalog.contextWindowFor` exists (`ModelCatalog.kt:75`) and is already called for the
usage payload (`TurnDriver.kt:752`) and launch recipes (`Daemon.kt:726`).
`gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/UpstreamFailureClassifier.kt:22-27,124-127` already
knows how to rewrite an overflow into Claude Code's compaction-trigger phrase — but only reactively,
after the 400. The retry-shaped compaction terminal is
`gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/pipeline/TurnPipeline.kt:74-80`, and nothing keys an
attempt count off `TurnMeta.conversationKey` (`Turn.kt:128`).

**Proposal** Two moves. (1) **Pre-dispatch**: estimate input tokens once at build time (reusing CX-03's
corrected estimator) and when the estimate exceeds the head's window, fail *before* the upstream call
with the overflow-classified message the classifier already produces — same signal, seconds earlier,
zero quota burned. (2) **Break the loop**: record the compact attempt against `TurnMeta.conversationKey`
in `CompactStats` and, on the second consecutive `empty_model`/overflow compact for that key, emit a
terminal that does *not* say "retry" and names the manual escape ("compaction cannot fit this transcript
— start a new session or reduce context"). Never hand Claude Code a retry instruction for a deterministic
failure. This does not violate the locked no-transcript-shrinking / no-fake-summaries rules: it rejects
and reports, it never shrinks or invents.

**Effort** M. **Risk** **Hard dependency: CX-03 must land first.** The estimator today is
raw-bytes/3 and shipping the pre-flight rejector on it would reject every image-bearing turn. Gate the
rejection on `estimate > window` (not > 90%) and log every fire until the estimator is tuned against
reality. The second-attempt wording change is safe — it fires only after a failure already happened
twice.

**Verification** `HeadServerIntegrationTest.kt`: a body whose estimate exceeds the catalog window is
rejected before any upstream call (assert `mock.upstreamBodies` unchanged — the existing count_tokens
test already uses that shape). A `TurnPipeline`/`CompactStats` test: the second consecutive empty

### NF-03 — The whole-turn wall clock is unenforced outside an open stream

**Problem** `TurnWatchdog.totalCap` is the only whole-turn ceiling, but its poller is launched inside the
successful-response block and cancelled in that block's `finally` — so it only runs while an upstream
stream is actually open. During connect, headers-wait, retry backoff, refresh, and between fold/re-anchor
rounds, nothing enforces the cap. `deadlineExceeded(t0)` is a per-`post()` budget (t0 captured per round),
so an N-round fold or re-anchor turn gets N × `upstreamTimeoutMs` of retry budget against a single
`totalCap`. The consequence is not just a slow turn: it holds its `InflightGate` slot the whole time, and
with a correctly small per-account `maxInflight` (NF-02) one wedged turn is a meaningful fraction of the
head's capacity.

**Evidence** Envoy separates per-try timeout from route timeout precisely so the route timeout bounds
the whole retried operation ([timeouts FAQ](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/faq/configuration/timeouts)).
Caddy's `lb_try_duration` bounds total retry time independently of `lb_try_interval`. Polly's documented
composition rule is that a timeout must *wrap* the retry to bound total time, never sit inside it
(https://www.pollydocs.org/strategies/timeout). `QwenLM/qwen-code#741` is the user-visible symptom of
getting this wrong: an opaque 483s wall that was retries × per-attempt timeout with no knob describing
the real budget.

**splice today** `gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/head/TurnDriver.kt:462` launches the
poller inside the response block (`val poller = drive.watchdog.launchIn(self, drive.slot, turnJob)`) and

### NF-04 — `Retry-After` in HTTP-date form is silently discarded

**Problem** `retryAfterMs` parses only the integer-seconds form; RFC 7231 also defines the HTTP-date
form, and any date-form header returns null. Three things then go wrong at once: the server's pushback
is not used as a backoff floor (G3's whole point), the >15s absurd-pushback give-up cannot fire, and the
429 cooldown falls back to the 20s guess instead of the server's actual reset. Cloudflare and several
gateway fronts in front of model backends emit the date form; splice would hammer a curve the server
explicitly told it not to.

**Evidence** qwen-code's `getRetryAfterDelayMs` (`packages/core/src/utils/retryPolicy.ts`) parses **both**
strict RFC7231 decimal-seconds and HTTP-date, and deliberately rejects loose numeric shapes JS `Number()`
would accept — the exact discipline splice half-has. LiteLLM honours `retry_after`/`x-retry-after` headers
as an override on local config. codex prefers `err.retry_delay()` over local backoff whenever the server
supplied one (`codex-rs/core/src/responses_retry.rs:126`).
[MDN's Retry-After reference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Headers/Retry-After)
documents both forms as equally normative.

**splice today** `gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/UpstreamClient.kt:360-362` —
`/** Seconds-form Retry-After → ms; HTTP-date form and garbage → null (backoff curve decides). */` over
`header?.trim()?.toLongOrNull()?.takeIf { it >= 0 }?.times(MS_PER_S)`. Three consumers inherit the null:
cooldown arming (`:407-408`, falling back to the 20s `DEFAULT_RATE_LIMIT_COOLDOWN_MS`), the
absurd-pushback give-up (`:418-422`), and the backoff floor (`:425` → `applyBackoff` at `:273-290`). G3
is marked `done` but landed only the seconds-form half; the shipped comment documents the date branch as
unhandled.

**Proposal** Extend `retryAfterMs` to fall back to
`java.time.format.DateTimeFormatter.RFC_1123_DATE_TIME` when `toLongOrNull()` fails, converting to a
delta against the wall clock and clamping negatives to 0. Keep strict-numeric-first ordering so nothing
on the existing path changes, and keep garbage → null. ~8 lines; all three consumers inherit the fix.

**Effort** S. **Risk** Very low. A skewed client clock could compute a wrong delta — clamp to
`[0, MAX]` and let NF-01's ceiling bound the top. **Wall-clock, not `MonoClock`, is required here**
because the header is an absolute time; note that explicitly in the code so a future reviewer does not
"fix" it to monotonic.

**Verification** Extend `gateway/provider-spi/src/test/kotlin/UpstreamClientRetryPolicyTest.kt` (which
already pins seconds-form at ~`:97-186`): date-form-honoured-as-floor, date-form-in-the-past-clamps-to-zero,

### NF-05 — G22 (tracked, still `todo`): no aggregate retry budget across turns

**Problem** Already tracked as **G22** in `dev/research/gateway-gaps-tracker.md:57`, status `todo`
("only if fan-out grows"). What specifically remains: the 429 case is solved by the shared cooldown, but
every *other* retryable class (500/502/503/504/408 and all transport classes) retries per-turn with no
cross-turn coordination. A sustained 5xx brownout across N concurrent turns produces N × `maxRetries`
upstream requests against a backend that is already failing — the textbook retry storm, and the shape
that pushes an account toward provider-side abuse detection. **Fan-out has grown**: `maxInflight`
defaults to 100 (`Knob.kt:139`), `maxQueued` to 512, and Claude Code fans out subagents, so G22's own
gating condition is now met.

**Evidence** Envoy's

(percent-of-active-requests with `min_retry_concurrency`) is the canonical shape. AWS SDK
standard/adaptive retry uses a 500-token bucket charging 14 tokens per transient retry and 5 per
throttling retry so retries stop system-wide around a ~22% failure rate. Live incidents of the
un-budgeted version: `anomalyco/opencode#17648` (~700 API calls in 5 seconds), `Aider-AI/aider#4241` and

**splice today** `gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/UpstreamClient.kt:76` holds the only

per-turn and never aggregated.

**Proposal** Add a per-`UpstreamClient` (= per-head = per-account) token bucket alongside

(`min_retry_concurrency`). Consult it in `statusPlan`/`onTransportError`: when empty, downgrade BACKOFF
to GIVE_UP with a distinct `onRetry` line ("retry budget exhausted for this head") so the give-up is
greppable and honest rather than silent. Do **not** touch the 429 path — it already fails fast. Surface
the bucket level through NF-10's health snapshot. Portkey's `retryHandler.ts` decrementing
`MAX_RETRY_LIMIT_MS` is the ~30-line reference implementation of the same idea in the time domain.

**Effort** M. **Risk** A per-head budget shared by concurrent turns means one turn's retries can consume
another's — the floor must guarantee a solo turn is never starved, or splice trades a storm for spurious
single-turn failures. Tune conservatively (generous bucket, hard floor); ship the counter observable-only
for one release if preferred.

**Verification** New `gateway/provider-spi/src/test/kotlin/UpstreamClientRetryBudgetTest.kt`: (a) a

### NF-09 — An armed cooldown answers HTTP 200 + an SSE error frame instead of a real 429

**Problem** When the head-wide cooldown is armed, `failFastIfRateLimited` correctly spends zero upstream

earliest `NextRecoverAt` (`sdk/cliproxy/auth/selector.go:396-457`). `CLIProxyAPI#4348` is a user asking
for precisely "429 + Retry-After instead of a bare timeout".

**splice today** `gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/UpstreamClient.kt:295-304` throws
`UpstreamFailed(..., RATE_LIMITED)` from inside the drive; the 200 was committed at

admission failures at `HeadServer.kt:299-330` → `respondAtCapacity` (`:505-511`). The pattern exists and

**Proposal** Expose `UpstreamClient.rateLimitedForMs(): Long` (0 = clear) and consult it in

**Effort** S. **Risk** Adds a HeadServer dependency on UpstreamClient state (today HeadServer holds it as
an opaque `deps.upstream`) — keep the surface to one read-only accessor. The pre-flight check introduces

### SH-06 — `CredentialLock` blocks a real thread forever on a live peer

**Problem** `withFileLock` calls `channel.lock()` with no timeout. The header comment justifies this with
"POSIX auto-releases the lock if the holding process dies, so a crashed peer can't wedge it — no timeout
knob needed", which is true only for a **dead** peer. A **live but slow** peer is the real case: the
refresh network call runs *inside* the lock, and `authHttpClient`'s 30s socket/request timeouts × the
3-attempt refresh loop plus 2s+4s backoff means a peer can legitimately hold the lock for ~96s. During
that window every `credentials()` call on this daemon that reaches the blocking stale-floor tier parks on
`Dispatchers.IO`, and Claude Code sees a stalled turn with no log line explaining why. The lock has no
observability either — nothing records that it waited, or for how long.

**Evidence** kimi-cli — the upstream of splice's own kimi head — made the opposite tradeoff explicitly:
`_CrossProcessLock.acquire_with_retry()` attempts 5 times with 1s+jitter backoff, makes one final
blocking attempt, and on failure returns `False` so the caller proceeds **without** the lock rather than
hanging (`src/kimi_cli/auth/oauth.py:280-350`; the surrounding TOCTOU work is PR
`MoonshotAI/kimi-cli#1996`). opencode's flock adds `staleMs` (60s default), `timeoutMs` (5min), jittered
retry, an `onWait` callback, and heartbeats (`packages/core/src/util/flock.ts`).

**splice today** `gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/CredentialLock.kt:48` —
`val lock = withContext(Dispatchers.IO) { channel.lock() }`, with the dead-peer justification at
`:44-47`. The network call is genuinely inside the lock on all three providers:
`KimiAuthProvider.kt:115`, `GrokAuthProvider.kt:172`, `CodexAuthProvider.kt:144` each do
`CredentialLock.withLock(authPath) { refreshLocked(...) }`, and `refreshLocked` → `exchangeRefreshToken`
→ `refreshCall` is the HTTP hop. Hold-duration arithmetic: `AuthHttpClient.kt:26-28` (10s connect / 30s
request / 30s socket) × `RefreshRetry.kt:19,68` (3 attempts, 2^attempt-second backoff).

**Proposal** Give `withLock` a deadline and an honest degrade path. Poll `channel.tryLock()` with jittered
backoff up to a `CREDENTIAL_LOCK_WAIT_MS` budget (default ~15s — comfortably above a healthy refresh RTT,
well below the pathological hold). On expiry, log
`[credential-lock] waited <n>ms for a peer on <path>; proceeding unlocked` and run the block anyway: G1's
other three layers (re-read inside the lock, peer-rotation adoption by token identity, one-shot
reread-on-rejection) all survive and are precisely the defence against the rare TOCTOU this admits, which
is why kimi-cli judged unlocked-and-bounded strictly better than hung. Emit a wait-duration counter so a
chronically contended path is visible rather than felt as latency. **This is an explicit operator fork:**
the alternative is to time out and return `RefreshOutcome.TransportFailed` instead of proceeding. Pick
one deliberately; do not leave it accidental.

**Effort** S. **Risk** Medium and worth stating plainly: this trades an unbounded hang for a rare
same-instant double refresh, the exact race G1 was written to close. The mitigation is that G1's
detection layers all remain.

**Verification** Extend `gateway/provider-spi/src/test/kotlin/CredentialLockTest.kt`: hold the sibling
`.lock` from a second `FileChannel` on another thread for longer than the budget; assert `withLock`
returns within budget+slack, the degrade line is logged, and the block still ran. Gate: `npm run gate`.

### NF-11 — A watchdog stall discards the round entirely

**Problem** splice already recovers mid-stream truncation: a `TurnOutcome.Failure` carrying a
`PartialRound` feeds `ReanchorRunner`, which re-POSTs a continuation and salvages the work. But a
**stall** — the same observable outcome, an incomplete stream — takes a completely different path:
`watchdogOutcome` builds a `Failure` with **no** partial, and both continuation gates short-circuit on
`signals.watchdogFired()`. So a turn that generated 4 minutes of reasoning and then went silent for the
idle cap loses everything and hands the user a retry, while a turn that got a truncated frame at the same
point is transparently continued. The structural reason is real — the watchdog cancels the turn coroutine,
so nothing may re-POST inside it — which means the fix is to change how the watchdog *signals*, not to
bolt on a salvage.

**Evidence** `QwenLM/qwen-code#5975`/`#4604`/`#4711`: a fixed inter-chunk idle watchdog firing during a
legitimately silent reasoning phase and hard-aborting the turn, reproducing 100% for some users while the
provider was still generating. `anomalyco/opencode#28957`/`#31456`/`#33714`: long reasoning killed
mid-response with the model "about to produce output". Both projects' users converge on the same
workaround — raise the timeout — because the harness offers no recovery, only abort.

**splice today** `gateway/dialect-openai-responses/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/responses/ResponsesStreamTranslator.kt:248-257`
(`watchdogOutcome` → `TurnOutcome.Failure(OVERLOADED, "...stream stalled...")` with no `partial=`), against
the sibling truncation branch at `:224-230` which passes `partial = partialOrNull(reducer)`. The in-repo
justification is verbatim at `:236-239`: "watchdog failures never carry one (their turn coroutine is being
cancelled — nothing may re-POST)". Both continuation gates refuse:
`gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/head/TurnDriver.kt:778` (`searchContinuation`) and `:904`
(`continuationForFailedRound`), each `signals.watchdogFired() || signals.clientGone() -> null`. The cancel
itself is `Watchdog.kt:81-84` (`firedRef.compareAndSet(null, breach); target.cancel()`) and makes **no**
Idle/TotalCap distinction — the two-tier split this needs does not exist yet.

**Proposal** Split the watchdog's two jobs. Keep `target.cancel()` for `TotalCap` (a hard ceiling must
kill). For `Idle` breaches, first set the sentinel and close only the **upstream read channel** —
cancelling the round's stream, not the turn job — let the translator produce its normal unfinished terminal
**with** the partial it already has, and let `ReanchorRunner` decide whether a continuation is worth one
more round (bounded by the existing re-anchor attempt budget and NF-03's turn-wide cap so it can never
loop). Drop the `watchdogFired()` short-circuit at `TurnDriver.kt:778`/`:904` for the Idle case only.

**Effort** L. **Risk** **Highest-risk item in this document.** The watchdog cancel is described in-repo
as "the only lock-safe kill" and the two-tier design exists because reaping prefill caused an
abort→retry→cold-re-read loop that ate a subscription. Re-anchoring after a stall re-POSTs into an upstream
that just proved it is stalling. The loop guard is NF-03's turn-wide cap plus the re-anchor budget, and
**both must be proven before this ships. Do not attempt this until NF-03 lands** — without a whole-turn
ceiling it is a token-burn machine.

**Verification** `gateway/provider-spi/src/test/kotlin/WatchdogTest.kt` must keep every existing case
green (prefill-not-reaped-before-first-byte, post-byte idle reap, total-cap reap) as the regression oracle.

### SH-01 — Codex treats a token with no decodable JWT `exp` as never-expiring (G18's unfixed twin)

**Problem** G18 fixed exactly this class on grok (a credential file with no usable expiry never
proactively refreshed and never aged out) by synthesizing `expiresAtMs = mtime + 4h`. The codex head has
the identical hole and no equivalent guard: when `decodeJwtClaims(access).long("exp")` yields null — an
opaque (non-JWT) access token, an `exp`-less JWT, an unparseable base64 — `expiresAtMs` is null and
`credentials()` returns the cached token *forever*. No proactive refresh ever fires; the head serves until
it 401s mid-turn, and the only thing that recovers is `UpstreamClient`'s reactive one-shot refresh. Kimi
has the mirror-image bug: `expiresAtS = obj.long("expires_at") ?: 0L` means an `expires_at`-less file
computes `remaining = -now`, lands below `HARD_FLOOR_S` on **every** call, and blocks on a refresh per
`credentials()` call. Three providers, three behaviours for the same input; only grok's is correct.

**Evidence** qwen-code's `TokenRefreshManager.needsRefresh()` deliberately refreshes proactively when
`expiresAt` is *missing*, documented as handling credentials stored before expiry tracking existed
(`packages/desktop/packages/shared/src/sources/token-refresh-manager.ts`). kimi-cli's `_refresh_threshold`
uses `max(300s, expires_in*0.5)` and never has an unbounded branch (`src/kimi_cli/auth/oauth.py:54-66`).
splice's own G18 is the in-repo precedent for the exact fix shape.

**splice today** `gateway/provider-codex/src/main/kotlin/splice/provider/codex/CodexAuthProvider.kt:115`
— `val expiresAtMs = decodeJwtClaims(access).long(FIELD_EXP)?.let { it * MS_PER_S }` (nullable, never
defaulted) feeding `:87` `if (expiresAt == null || expiresAt - clock() >= PROACTIVE_WINDOW_MS) current`.
Contrast `gateway/provider-grok/src/main/kotlin/splice/provider/grok/GrokAuthProvider.kt:134` —
`?.let { it.copy(expiresAtMs = it.expiresAtMs ?: (mtime + SYNTHETIC_EXPIRY_TTL_MS)) }`. Kimi:

failure (`:93`). **Not covered by G6 or G18**: G6 covered only the JWT-`exp`-present path on codex; G18 is
grok-only.

**Proposal** Give all three providers **one** missing-expiry policy. In `CodexAuthProvider.readSnapshot()`,

operator can see the auth.json shape drifted). In `KimiAuthProvider.parseSnapshot()`, replace `?: 0L` with

**Effort** S. **Risk** Low. The synthesized ceiling is only ever a floor on staleness — it can force an
extra refresh, never suppress one. On a healthy codex file (JWT with `exp`) the branch is unreachable, so
behaviour is unchanged. Kimi's change *reduces* refresh volume.

**Verification** New cases in `gateway/provider-codex/src/test/kotlin/CodexAuthTest.kt`: an auth.json whose

### SH-02 — Refresh-ineffective loop on grok burns rotating refresh tokens

**Problem** Nothing checks that a refresh actually advanced the expiry the next read will see. On grok,
`expiresIn` from the token endpoint is nullable; when absent, `persistRotation` computes
`expiresAtMs = null`, and `mergedAuthJson`'s `replacedTopLevel()` then **carries over** the old
(already-in-window or past) `expires` value rather than replacing it. The write succeeds, the cache is
invalidated, `RefreshOutcome.Refreshed` is returned — and the very next `credentials()` re-reads the same
stale `expires`, lands below `STALE_FLOOR_MS` again, and blocks on another refresh. Every request. Because
grok rotates refresh tokens, each iteration consumes and replaces a refresh token against `auth.x.ai` as
fast as turns arrive — precisely the token-burn shape that killed a credential on 2026-07-18. There is no
ceiling, no cooldown, and nothing logs "I refreshed and the expiry did not move".

**Evidence** CLIProxyAPI carries a purpose-built guard: `refreshIneffectiveBackoff` (30s) applied when an
executor's `Refresh()` reports success but the credential still evaluates as needing refresh, explicitly
to stop the auto-refresh loop burning CPU/quota in a tight success/re-check cycle
(`sdk/cliproxy/auth/conductor_refresh.go:486-490,954-956`). `anomalyco/opencode#17648`/`#21960` document
the sibling class (an uncapped branch producing ~700 API calls in 5 seconds).
`google-gemini/gemini-cli#21691` is the adjacent shape (OAuth refresh token lost during refresh).

**splice today** `gateway/provider-grok/src/main/kotlin/splice/provider/grok/GrokAuthProvider.kt:244`
(`val expiresAtMs = fresh.expiresIn?.let { clock() + it * MS_PER_S }`), with `GrokRefreshedTokens.expiresIn`
genuinely nullable (`:57`) populated from a nullable parse
(`gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/GrokRefresh.kt:80`). When null, `replacedTopLevel`
(`GrokAuthProvider.kt:277-278`) returns false for `FIELD_EXPIRES` so `mergedAuthJson` keeps the stale
value. The comment directly above at `:255-259` **already names this exact loop** for the other branch:
"`expires` is OVERWRITTEN when the refresh response carried expires_in — keeping the old value would leave
a stale past expiry that re-triggers the proactive refresh on every turn." The re-entering tier is
`:98-118`. `SingleFlight` dedups concurrent entrants but not sequential turns, and the only latch that
exists (`invalidGrantLatch`) fires on Rejected/invalid_grant, never on Refreshed. **Not covered by G15
(terminal invalid_grant) or G17 (two-tier prefetch)** — neither covers a *successful but ineffective*
refresh.

**Proposal — grok-scoped.** (a) In `GrokAuthProvider.persistRotation`, when the endpoint returned no
`expires_in`, synthesize `clock() + DEFAULT_ROTATION_TTL_MS` rather than preserving the stale on-disk value
— a token just minted is not older than the one it replaced. (b) Add a provider-level
`lastIneffectiveRefreshAtMs` guard: if `doRefresh()` returns Refreshed but a fresh `readSnapshot()` still
evaluates inside the blocking tier, log once (`[<tag>] refresh succeeded but expiry did not advance —
backing off <N>s`) and suppress further refreshes for `REFRESH_INEFFECTIVE_BACKOFF_MS` (30s), serving the
current token. (c) Count it: a new health counter so the dashboard shows nonzero "ineffective refreshes"
rather than the operator learning via provider-side token death.
**Do not mirror this to kimi.** Kimi's write path is not symmetric: `KimiRefreshedTokens.expiresIn` is a
non-nullable `Long` (`KimiAuthProvider.kt:37`), `kimiAuthJsonFromTokenResponse` hard-errors when the
endpoint omits `expires_in` (`KimiOAuth.kt:107`), and `kimiAuthJson` always writes a fresh
`expires_at = now/1000 + expiresIn` (`KimiOAuth.kt:118`). Kimi's `?: 0L` is a *read* default for a file
lacking the field; one successful refresh writes a real `expires_at` and self-heals (that read default is
SH-01's scope, not this one).

**Effort** M. **Risk** Medium. (b) deliberately serves a token the tier logic wanted refreshed, so a
genuinely near-expiry token could 401 during the 30s backoff — bounded and strictly better than the current
unbounded burn, and `UpstreamClient`'s reactive 401-refresh still covers it. (a) changes on-disk `expires`

### SH-03 — The auth probe reports healthy for a latched credential and for any serve-stale state

**Problem** `probeOnce()` defines health as `credentials() != null`. For all three OAuth providers,
`credentials()` returns the current token when a refresh fails but the token has not hard-expired
(`refreshed ?: (if (clock() < expiresAt) current else null)`), and `doRefresh()` short-circuits to
`Rejected(invalid_grant)` without any network call once the `InvalidGrantLatch` is armed. So a credential
whose refresh token is **confirmed dead** — the one state the daemon knows with certainty and already
surfaces as `refresh_latched` in `describe()` — reports healthy to the probe, logs nothing, and transitions
nothing. Same for a run of failed refreshes short of `invalid_grant`: as long as the stale token has not
hard-expired, the probe is green. **Framed precisely**: a latched credential is not *dead at that instant*
— the access token still serves real turns until it expires. The honest statement of the gap is that the
daemon holds a **certain prediction of imminent auth death** and the probe, whose entire purpose is
pre-traffic warning, cannot express it.

**Evidence** `NousResearch/hermes-agent#70344`: after a network blip a gateway stayed in "running" state
with the connection never returning to "connected" for ~22h because the health/escalation state machine had
a branch it never re-evaluated — the process "looked healthy". CLIProxyAPI keeps a per-credential
per-model state machine (`QuotaState` + `ModelState`, `sdk/cliproxy/auth/`) whose availability is separate
from "a token string exists", and `clearUnauthorizedModelStates` explicitly resumes models suspended for an
unauthorized reason after a successful refresh (`conductor_refresh.go:817-836`).

**splice today** `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/AuthProbeLoop.kt:70-71` —
`var ok = auth.credentials() != null; if (!ok) ok = auth.refresh() != null`. Serve-stale:
`GrokAuthProvider.kt:117` `refreshed ?: (if (clock() < expiresAt) current else null)`, identical at
`CodexAuthProvider.kt:97` and `KimiAuthProvider.kt:93`. Latch short-circuit: `GrokAuthProvider.kt:170`
`if (invalidGrantLatch.isLatched(mtime)) return RefreshOutcome.Rejected(INVALID_GRANT_REASON)` — the same
state already rendered as `refresh_latched` at `:299`.

**Proposal** Stop inferring health from a nullable token. Add `suspend fun health(): AuthHealth` to
`RefreshableAuthProvider` (default impl derives today's behaviour so non-OAuth providers are unaffected),
returning a small sealed value: `Healthy(expiresInMs)` | `Stale(expiresInMs, lastRefreshFailure)` |
`Dead(reason)` — where `Dead` is exactly the latched / NoRefreshToken / NoCredentialsFile set the providers
already compute. `AuthProbeLoop` switches on that instead of `!= null`, so a latch flips healthy→unhealthy
on the first tick after arming and a serve-stale run logs a distinct STALE transition. This is the
precondition for SH-08 (surfacing) and SH-09 (doctor).

**Effort** M. **Risk** Low-medium: a new SPI method touches four providers plus test fakes — the same
"breaking interface change across 3 modules" shape the tracker's L3-phase-2 row deferred once. A defaulted
interface implementation keeps the blast radius to the three OAuth providers.

**Verification** Extend `gateway/app/src/test/kotlin/AuthProbeLoopTest.kt` with a fake whose

`npm run gate`.

### SH-04 — The auth probe loop is not supervised

**Problem** The probe body is wrapped in `runCatchingCancellable`, which catches **only** `IOException`,
`SerializationException`, and `IllegalArgumentException`. Any other `RuntimeException` raised inside
`probeOnce()` — an `IllegalStateException` from a provider `check`/`error`, an NPE, a ktor engine exception
type not in that list — escapes the `while (isActive)` loop and terminates the launched coroutine.
`probeScope` is a `SupervisorJob` so siblings survive, but nothing restarts *this* head's probe, nothing
logs its death, and `start()` refuses to re-arm because `job != null`. The daemon then runs for its whole
lifetime with a permanently dead pre-traffic auth check for that head, while `/health` still reports the
head running. A self-healing mechanism that cannot heal itself is the worst kind of green.

**Evidence** `NousResearch/hermes-agent#70344` (the reconnect/escalation watcher that never restarts after
a plain connection loss; the process reports healthy for 22h until an external restart). Erlang/OTP
supervision is the canonical counter-pattern: a permanent child is always restarted, with intensity limits
(max N restarts in P seconds) — mirrored by systemd's `StartLimitBurst`/`StartLimitIntervalSec`.

**splice today** `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/AuthProbeLoop.kt:55-61`
(`job = scope.launch { while (isActive) { runCatchingCancellable { probeOnce() }.onFailure { log(...) };
delay(intervalMs) } }`), `:54` (`if (job != null) return`). Catch list:
`gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/util/Cancellation.kt:22-26`. Supervisor: `Daemon.kt:278`
(`probeScope = CoroutineScope(Dispatchers.Default + SupervisorJob())`) — isolates siblings, restarts
nothing. `probeOnce()` at `:70-71` calls `auth.credentials()` and `auth.refresh()`, both provider code that
can `check`/`error`. G8 landed the loop; nothing in the tracker covers its supervision.

**Proposal** Make the loop self-restarting under an intensity limit. Attach
`job.invokeOnCompletion { cause -> if (cause != null && cause !is CancellationException) {
log("[auth-probe:$key] loop died: $cause — restarting (n/max)"); restart() } }`, with a bounded restart
budget (e.g. 5 restarts per 10 minutes, systemd's `StartLimitBurst` shape) after which it logs a terminal
"probe permanently down" line that SH-08 surfaces on `/health`. Independently, widen the per-tick guard to
a narrowly-scoped local combinator catching `RuntimeException` for the probe body only — the same technique
and justification G28 used for `catchingTurnFailure` in `TurnDriver.kt` — so a tick failure is a logged
tick, not a dead loop.

**Effort** S. **Risk** Low. The restart budget prevents a hot restart loop; the widened catch is scoped to
the probe body and needs the same detekt `TooGenericExceptionCaught` treatment G28 already established
precedent for (a local combinator, never widening the shared primitive).

**Verification** New `AuthProbeLoopTest` case: a fake provider whose `credentials()` throws
`IllegalStateException` on tick 1 — assert the loop logs the death, restarts, and ticks again (today the
test would see zero further ticks); plus a case proving the budget terminates after N and logs the
permanent-down line. Gate: `npm run gate`.

### SH-05 — No cooldown on repeated non-`invalid_grant` refresh failure

**Problem** `InvalidGrantLatch` suppresses re-POSTing only for a **confirmed** `invalid_grant`. Every other
terminal rejection — `Rejected` with a non-invalid_grant reason, `Denied("HTTP 400")`, "refresh retries
exhausted", `TransportFailed`, `PersistFailed` — has no memory at all. Two amplifiers compound: (1)
`AuthProbeLoop` calls `refresh()` on every tick whenever `credentials()` is null, and each `refresh()` runs
`refreshWithRetry`'s 3 attempts with 2s/4s backoff, so a dead-but-not-invalid_grant endpoint gets 3 POSTs a
minute forever; (2) in the prefetch tier every `credentials()` call does
`prefetchScope.launch { singleFlight.run { ... } }`, and since `SingleFlight` never reuses a settled wave,
a persistently failing refresh produces a fresh 3-attempt cycle **per `credentials()` call** — and
`UpstreamClient` calls `credentials()` once per attempt, per turn. Under a provider-side outage that is the
shape that gets an account rate-limited or flagged, and nothing notices or slows down.

**Evidence** qwen-code's `TokenRefreshManager` tracks `failedAttempts` per source-slug with a 5-minute
cooldown and skips refresh entirely while cooling (`rateLimited:true`) rather than hammering a broken OAuth
endpoint. LiteLLM's ChatGPT authenticator gates repeat device-code attempts behind
`DEVICE_CODE_COOLDOWN_SECONDS=300` after a refresh failure (`litellm/llms/chatgpt/authenticator.py`).
Hermes uses failure-class-differentiated cooldown TTLs (401 → 5min, 429/402 → 1h) in
`agent/credential_pool.py:117-123`; `hermes-agent#70401` is the failure: uncapped credential rotation on a
persistent 401 became a non-interruptible loop only an external kill stopped.

**splice today** `gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/auth/InvalidGrantLatch.kt` is a single
mtime-keyed boolean armed only on `outcome.reason == INVALID_GRANT_REASON` (kimi `:117`, grok `:174`, codex
`:146`). `AuthProbeLoop.kt:56-61` ticks on a fixed interval and calls `refresh()` unconditionally when
`credentials()` is null. `RefreshRetry.kt:57-74` runs `maxAttempts` per *call* with no cross-call state.
`gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/SingleFlight.kt:41-42` comments "reuse only a still-running

uncovered set is still everything else.)

**Proposal** Generalize `InvalidGrantLatch` into a `RefreshCooldown` in `:core`, keyed like the latch on the
auth-file mtime so a re-login clears it automatically. On each terminal non-Granted outcome record
`(reason-class, consecutive-failure count, nextAttemptAtMs)` with escalating backoff: a short leash for
transport/5xx (30s → 1m → 5m capped) and a long one for endpoint rejections (5m capped, matching qwen-code),

position the latch is consulted today (before any file read or network call), returning
`RefreshOutcome.Rejected("cooling down: <reason>, <n>s remaining")` so `credentialsOrNull` still owns the

**Effort** M. **Risk** Medium: too long a cooldown delays recovery from a genuinely transient outage.
Mitigated by (a) escalation from a 30s floor rather than a flat 5m, (b) mtime-keyed auto-clear on any
re-login, (c) an explicit decision on whether `UpstreamClient`'s reactive one-shot 401 refresh may bypass
the cooldown once per turn — decide it, do not leave it accidental.

**Verification** New test alongside `gateway/core/src/test/kotlin/InvalidGrantLatchTest.kt` pinning the

Gate: `npm run gate`.

### SH-07 — Nothing verifies a token against the provider (G8's stated problem, half-closed)

**Problem** Tracked as **G8**, marked `done` — and the loop, transition logging, and single-flight wiring
genuinely landed and are well tested. What did **not** land is the part of G8's own problem statement that
reads "revoked-but-unexpired tokens invisible until a real turn fails"
(`dev/research/gateway-best-practices-2026-07-18.md:46`). The landed probe's health signal is a **local
file check**: for all three OAuth providers `credentials()` returns a cached snapshot without touching the
network unless the token is already inside its proactive window. Revoke the ChatGPT / xAI / Moonshot grant
from the provider's own dashboard and the probe sees a parseable, not-locally-expired file, logs nothing,
and reports healthy for the full remaining token lifetime. The half G6/G17/G18 closed was local unawareness
of expiry; the revocation trigger is still open.

**Evidence** Gemini CLI verifies against the server rather than trusting local expiry: `initOauthClient()`
calls `getAccessToken()` + `getTokenInfo()` on each cached credential specifically to check server-side
revocation (`packages/core/src/code_assist/oauth2.ts:129-230`). `router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI#3654`, `#1015`,
`#4120` document the operational cost of not doing this: OAuth credentials going 401/token_invalidated on a
provider-side risk-control action, recurring weekly, indistinguishable from a proxy bug. LiteLLM ships
`background_health_checks` that periodically probe every configured deployment
(`litellm/proxy/health_check.py`).

**splice today** `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/AuthProbeLoop.kt:69-77` (`probeOnce` is
`credentials()`-then-`refresh()`, never an upstream call); `CodexAuthProvider.kt:87-88`,
`GrokAuthProvider.kt:105-106`, `KimiAuthProvider.kt:75-79` all short-circuit to the cached snapshot outside
the proactive window.

**Proposal** Add an optional `suspend fun verifyUpstream(): Boolean?` to `RefreshableAuthProvider` (null =
"this provider has no cheap verification endpoint", keeping api-key heads and future providers free).
Implement per OAuth provider against the cheapest authenticated endpoint that provider actually offers (a
models list / introspection GET chosen from what the head's `base_url` already exposes), with its own short
timeout on `authHttpClient`. `AuthProbeLoop` calls it on a much slower cadence than the local tick (every
Nth tick, default ~10 minutes), so the cost is one cheap request per head per 10 minutes, feeding the same
transition log and SH-03's health state. An auth-shaped verification failure arms SH-05's cooldown and
marks the head `Dead(revoked)` rather than silently retrying.

**Effort** M. **Risk** Medium — and this is the one item in §6 that could be net-negative if done
carelessly. It adds real background traffic to a provider on an account that may itself be quota-metered,
and picks a per-provider endpoint that could change. Must be knob-gated (default on, disable-able), must
never count against a turn's admission or retry budget, and must not itself trip the provider-side abuse
heuristics referenced in `MoonshotAI/kimi-cli#2077` (gateway UA filtering) — reuse the head's own identity
headers, not a new client shape.

**Verification** Per-provider tests asserting `verifyUpstream()` maps 401/403 to `Dead` and 200 to
`Healthy` without touching the credential file; an `AuthProbeLoopTest` case asserting the slow cadence
(verification fires on tick N, not every tick). Gate: `npm run gate` plus a manual pass of
`checks/e2e/heads-e2e.sh` against a live head.

### SH-08 — Auth health, time-to-expiry, and cooldown state never leave the process

**Problem** The daemon computes, per head and per tick, exactly the facts an operator needs — whether the
probe says healthy, how long until the token expires (grok/codex/kimi all compute `expiresAt` to decide
their tiers), and whether the `invalid_grant` latch is armed. Almost none of it escapes.
`AuthDescription.fields` today carries `auth_path` and `login` (grok/kimi) plus `account_id_masked` and
`last_refresh` (codex) and `refresh_latched` — but **nothing time- or health-bearing**: no expiry, no probe
health, no cooldown remaining. `/health` reports only ok/version/heads/readyHeads/failedHeads with no auth
dimension; the probe's healthy↔unhealthy transitions go to daemon.log and nowhere else. So "is my kimi
token about to need a re-login?" and "is my grok head silently about to die?" are both answered by grepping
a log file. This is the surfacing half of SH-03/SH-05/SH-07 — without it those states self-heal invisibly,
which is only half the goal.

**Evidence** codex pushes rate-limit/account state to clients proactively as typed notifications rather
than making them poll or fail (`AccountRateLimitsUpdatedNotification` / `RateLimitSnapshot` with
`usedPercent` and `resetsAt`, `codex-rs/app-server-protocol/schema/typescript/v2/`). qwen-code's
daemon-global deep health (`GET /health?deep=1`) aggregates real runtime state and fails **closed** to
`{status: degraded}` rather than reporting a partial snapshot (`docs/design/daemon-global-deep-health.md`).
copilot-api and CLIProxyAPI both surface per-account credential/quota state in their management UIs.

**splice today** `gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/auth/Credentials.kt:19-23`

`webui/src/pages/auth/index.tsx`.

**Proposal** Extend `AuthDescription` with three typed, non-secret fields — `expiresInMs: Long?`,
`health: AuthHealth` (from SH-03), `cooldownRemainingMs: Long?` (from SH-05) — populated by each OAuth
provider from values it already computes. Add an `auth` block to `controlHealthJson` summarising counts
(healthy / stale / dead heads) so the shim's existing `/health` poll and any script can see it without a
bearer round trip. Render it on the dashboard's auth page as a per-head expiry countdown plus a health chip,
and let the fleet banner name a head that is stale or dead at rest — the banner mechanism already exists for
usage warn/critical.

**Effort** M. **Risk** Low. Additive wire fields; nothing secret is added (expiry and health are not
credential material). `gateway/control/src/test/kotlin/splice/control/WebuiContractTest.kt:126` pins the
`health` object by field name, so the contract test must move in the same commit.

**Verification** `ControlServerTest` assertions that `/api/auth` carries `expiresInMs`/`health` for an
OAuth head and omits `expiresInMs` for api-key heads; a `controlHealthJson` case asserting the auth summary

### SH-12 / JW-18 — MgmtKey silently mints a new bearer on ANY read failure

**Problem** `MgmtKey.ensure()` wraps the read of the existing key file in
`runCatching { ... }.discard("unreadable/empty key file falls through to regeneration below")` and falls
through to generating 32 fresh random bytes. The discard reason is honest about *what* it does but conflates
two very different situations: "no key file yet" (mint — correct) and "the key file exists but I could not
read it" (a permissions change, a partial read, a disk-pressure event) where minting silently revokes the
dashboard session, any script holding the key, and the launch shim's ability to stop a stale daemon. The
operator's symptom is mysterious 401s from the control plane with nothing anywhere saying "the mgmt key was
regenerated at time T because X". doctor's mgmt-key check reports presence, which is true — of the brand-new
key.

**Evidence** `router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI#4433`: a mutate-on-first-run secret rewrite (plaintext key hashed
and written back) combined with a path/binary mismatch produced 401s that "look like a proxy bug"; the
maintainer could only close it by proving which config the running process had loaded. Portainer's first-run
setup-token model exists specifically so an unclaimed/regenerated credential state is explicit rather than
inferred. Git's `--show-origin`/`--show-scope` is the general counter-pattern: never change auth-bearing
state without recording where and why.

**splice today** `gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/config/MgmtKey.kt:19-25` (the
`runCatching`/`discard` fallthrough) → `:26-29` (unconditional mint + `SecureFile.writeAtomic0600`). The
header comment at `:1-3` claims the key is "minted ONCE", which the code contradicts. Doctor's view:
`DoctorCommand.kt:155-168` (`mgmtKeyCheck` does `Files.readString(keyFile).trim().isNotEmpty()` and returns
OK on present). Consumers that break on rotation: `RestartCommand.kt:17-28` and `bin/splice-launch:156-157`
/ `:106-116` (the stale-daemon shutdown POST). Note this is a **documented L2 decision** (the `.discard(why)`
annotation), not an oversight — the fix is to split the two cases, keeping the quiet path for first run.

**Proposal** Distinguish absent from unreadable. `NoSuchFileException` → mint quietly (first run, expected).
Any other throwable, or a present-but-blank/short file → mint **and** emit a loud one-line warning naming
the path, the exception class, and the consequence ("every existing bearer token is now invalid; re-copy the
key from \<path\>"), plus record a `mgmtKeyMintedAtMs` exposed on `/api/status` so doctor can report "key
minted 3m ago" when it is suspiciously fresh relative to daemon uptime. **Operator fork:** on an
unreadable-but-present file, the alternative is to fail daemon start rather than mint — a silently rotated
bearer on a loopback single-user daemon is arguably a worse outcome than a loud refusal. Pick one
deliberately and write the reason into the file header comment.

**Effort** S. **Risk** Low. The logging half is free. The fail-instead-of-mint half changes boot behaviour
and should be the operator's explicit choice, not assumed. `MgmtKey` lives in `:core`, which is subject to
the no-println wall — the warning must go through an injected logger (a small constructor-signature change:
`MgmtKey` currently takes only `statePaths`), not `System.err`, or it trips the gate.

**Verification** New `gateway/core/src/test/kotlin/MgmtKeyTest.kt` (none exists today; the key is exercised
only indirectly via `ControlServerTest`/`BearerTokenTest` bearer matching): absent file mints silently;
unreadable file mints AND invokes the logger once with the path; blank file is treated as unreadable, not as
a valid key. Gate: `npm run gate`.

### SH-14 — daemon.log rotation can wedge permanently

**Problem** `persistentLogger` tracks `written` in memory and rotates at 64MB by moving daemon.log to
daemon.log.1. If that `Files.move` throws — the file was removed by an external logrotate, the state dir
went read-only, a permissions change — control falls into the `onFailure` branch, which closes and nulls the
writer but leaves `written` at its ≥MAX value. Every subsequent line therefore re-enters the rotate branch,
throws again **before ever reaching `Files.newBufferedWriter`**, and never writes. daemon.log stops receiving
anything, forever, with no error surfaced anywhere — the stderr tee still runs, but on a nohup'd daemon
stderr goes to `/dev/null`. The whole point of adding rotation (the 2026-07-18 "daemon.log grew forever"
finding) was a bounded, always-present log; this failure produces the unbounded-in-the-other-direction
result: no log at all. This compounds JW-01: with boot stderr discarded *and* the rotate wedge silent, a
daemon can be simultaneously unlogged and undiagnosable.

**Evidence** s6/runit's supervision model treats log delivery as a first-class guaranteed service (a
dedicated logger process per service) precisely because a logger that silently stops is indistinguishable
from a quiet system. splice's own `JsonlSink` is the in-repo counter-example: the same append+rotate-at-
maxBytes logic, but tested (`gateway/core/src/test/kotlin/JsonlSinkTest.kt`) and stateless with respect to a
cached byte count.

**splice today** `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/Main.kt:121-147` — `written` seeded once from
`Files.size(file)` at `:126`; the rotate branch at `:132-137` does
`writer?.close(); Files.move(file, rolled, REPLACE_EXISTING); writer = null; written = 0L`; the `onFailure`
at `:142-145` resets **only** `writer`, never `written`. The stderr tee at `:138` goes to `/dev/null`
(`bin/splice-launch:136`, `AdminSupport.kt:125`). Repo-wide grep for `persistentLogger` returns only its
definition (`:121`) and its single call site (`Main.kt:50`); there is no `MainTest`.

**Proposal** Stop caching the size, or reconcile it on every failure. **Preferred**: route daemon.log
through the already-tested `JsonlSink` rotate primitive (or extract that primitive into a shared
`RotatingWriter` in `:core`) so there is one rotate implementation with one test suite instead of two
implementations and one test suite. **Minimum viable** if that refactor is too wide: in the `onFailure`
branch recompute `written = Files.size(file)` (defaulting to 0 when the file is gone) so a failed rotate
self-corrects on the next line, and emit one stderr line naming the rotate failure so a wedged logger is not
silent.

**Effort** S. **Risk** Low. The size recompute is one line and strictly restores forward progress. The
`JsonlSink` consolidation is the better outcome but touches the daemon boot path — land it with the test in
the same commit.

**Verification** New `MainTest` exercising `persistentLogger` against a temp dir: force a rotate failure by
deleting daemon.log after `written` crosses the cap, then assert the next line **is** written (today it is
not) and that the rotate-failure line was emitted; plus a case pinning rotation at `MAX_LOG_BYTES`. Gate:
`npm run gate`.

### CX-10 — No repair path for `tool_use`/`tool_result` pairing

**Problem** The Responses input builder emits a `function_call` for every `ToolUseBlock` and a
`function_call_output` for every `ToolResultBlock`, in transcript order, with **no check that the pair
exists**. If Claude Code ever sends a `tool_result` whose `tool_use` is absent — its own history trimming,
an interrupted turn, a future block kind splice drops as `UnknownBlock` (CX-04) — the Responses API rejects
the request with a deterministic 400, and because 400 is correctly non-retryable, **every subsequent turn in
that session repeats the same rejection**. splice already has a one-shot amend hook that repairs two
specific shape-400s; pairing is not one of them, so the session is wedged until the user starts a new one.

**Evidence** `anomalyco/opencode#25774` ("Anthropic normalize splits tool-call from tool-result causing
dangling tool_use error"), `#5750`, `#31048`: message normalization **and** compaction independently produce
an assistant `tool_use` with no immediately-following `tool_result`, "Anthropic rejects the whole request",
"permanently wedging the session". `QwenLM/qwen-code#656`/`#528`: "An assistant message with tool_calls must
be followed by tool messages responding to each tool_call_id" — surfaces on every subsequent message once
the transcript is in that state. `google-gemini/gemini-cli#26956` (turn-ordering 400s).

**splice today** `gateway/dialect-openai-responses/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/responses/ResponsesRequestBuilder.kt:635-659`
(`appendToolResult` emits `function_call_output` with `put("call_id", block.toolUseId)` unconditionally) and
`:565-597` (`appendToolUse` likewise). The amend whitelist is
`gateway/dialect-openai-responses/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/responses/ResponsesProvider.kt:166-172` —
exactly two branches (`isEncryptedContentError` → strip stale reasoning; `isToolSurfaceRejection` → drop the
tool_search tool). The repo **does** carry orphan-awareness for the *reasoning* family
(`ResponsesRequestBuilder.kt:584` comment: "the API rejects both an orphaned reasoning item and a
function_call whose reasoning was dropped"; `ToolSurfaceRecovery.kt:7-11`; `ReasoningCache.kt:139-153`),
which proves the class is understood and the tool-pairing guard was simply never built.

**Proposal** Two layers. (1) **Prevention, build-time**: while walking messages, collect the set of emitted
`call_id`s; drop (and count via CX-04's counter, plus one log line) any `function_call_output` whose call_id
was never emitted, and conversely drop a `function_call` for which no output ever appears AND which is not
the last assistant turn. (2) **Recovery**: add a third branch to `amendBodyOnFailure` keyed on a broad
status-4xx plus a pairing-specific wording match ("tool call", "function call output", "no tool output") —
same deliberately-broad style as `isToolSurfaceRejection` — that strips the offending items from `input` and
re-POSTs once through the existing `amendedOnce` budget.

### NF-15 — Cooldown expiry releases every waiting turn at once

**Problem** The head-wide cooldown is a pure time horizon: at the instant `clock() >= rateLimitedUntilMs`,
every queued and incoming turn passes `failFastIfRateLimited` simultaneously and hits the upstream together.
If the account is still limited they all 429, each re-arms the horizon, and the cycle repeats — a
self-sustaining sawtooth. There is also no early recovery: an account that clears in 3s still blocks for the
full 20s default because nothing ever probes.

**Evidence** Envoy outlier detection and every classical circuit breaker use a **half-open** state admitting
a limited number of trial requests before fully re-opening
(https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/intro/arch_overview/upstream/outlier). resilience4j exposes this
as `permittedNumberOfCallsInHalfOpenState`. CLIProxyAPI's window-based escalation absorbs in-window failures
without re-escalating, which is the complementary half of the same idea.

**splice today** `gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/UpstreamClient.kt:295-304`
(`failFastIfRateLimited`) is a bare `rateLimitedUntilMs.get() - clock() <= 0` check with no admission
limiting, no probe token, no half-open state; `:406-413` re-arms unconditionally on any observed 429 via
`accumulateAndGet(until, ::maxOf)` and then GIVE_UPs. Default cooldown is 20s (`:600`
`DEFAULT_RATE_LIMIT_COOLDOWN_MS = 20_000L`). Grep for probe/halfOpen in the file returns only unrelated
hits. Not covered by G20 (passive counters) or G21 (bounded admission queue) — neither is a half-open
breaker. Herd size is plausible on this daemon: `maxInflight` defaults to 100 and the knob comment
explicitly cites "multi-agent fleets".

**Proposal** Add an `AtomicBoolean probeInFlight` next to `rateLimitedUntilMs`. Once the horizon expires,
exactly ONE turn wins `compareAndSet(false, true)` and proceeds as the probe; all others keep failing fast
with a distinct message ("probing upstream recovery") until the probe resolves. On probe success clear the
horizon and the flag; on probe 429 re-arm and clear the flag. ~20 lines, entirely inside `UpstreamClient`,
no new external state. This is also the natural gate for NF-22's "first observer" condition.

**Effort** S. **Risk** A probe that hangs for the full first-byte timeout would block recovery for other
turns — guard the flag with a timestamp so a stale probe is reclaimed after one poll-interval-ish window.
Slightly increases latency to full recovery for non-probe turns, bounded by one round trip.

**Verification** New cases in `UpstreamClientRetryPolicyTest.kt` using the injected clock: after the horizon
expires, N concurrent posts produce exactly ONE upstream call (MockEngine request count) until it resolves;
a successful probe clears the horizon for all; a 429 probe re-arms it. Gate: `npm run gate`.

### SH-13 — A deleted mgmt-key file is a documented manual-kill

**Problem** doctor's `mgmtKeyCheck` raises a hard FAIL when the key file is missing while the daemon is
running, and its remediation is literally "terminate the daemon process manually; the next launch re-mints
the key" — because the daemon holds its boot-minted key in memory, re-reads no file, and `splice restart`
cannot authenticate its own shutdown without the file. That is a self-inflicted manual intervention: the
process that owns the authoritative value is running, healthy, and one atomic 0600 write away from restoring
the file every other component reads. This is the clearest instance in the codebase of "a human must notice
and fix something the daemon already knows".

**Evidence** Homebrew's `brew doctor --fix-symlinks` is the canonical shape — a diagnostic that *repairs*
the class of breakage it detects rather than printing a manual procedure. Docker Desktop's self-diagnose
repairs stale certificates and missing components in the same spirit. `google-gemini/gemini-cli#26715` is
the counter-example this avoids: a corrupted credential file that crash-loops instead of degrading.

**splice today** `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/cli/DoctorCommand.kt:155-176` — `:164` is the
literal fix string "terminate the daemon process manually; the next launch re-mints the key" on a hard FAIL.
`gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/config/MgmtKey.kt:13` — `private val value: String by lazy {
ensure() }` holds the value for the process lifetime and `ensure()` (`:17-30`) is never re-entered.
`SecureFile.writeAtomic0600` is already the write primitive at `:26`.

**Proposal** Make the daemon reconcile its own key file. Cheapest form: a periodic (or probe-piggybacked)
check — if the daemon is running and `statePaths.mgmtKeyFile` is absent or blank, re-write the in-memory
value via `SecureFile.writeAtomic0600` and log
`[mgmt-key] key file missing at <path> — re-persisted from memory`. That turns doctor's FAIL into an INFO
the operator may never see, and removes the only "kill the process by hand" instruction in the whole
diagnostic. Keep doctor's FAIL for the case where the daemon is running AND the re-persist itself failed (a
genuinely unwritable state dir), which is the real unrecoverable state.

**Effort** S. **Risk** Low — writing a file the daemon already owns, atomically, at 0600. **Must not**
re-write when the file exists with different content; that would clobber a deliberate operator rotation.
Only the absent/blank case self-heals, matching `ensure()`'s own existing "unreadable/empty falls through"
semantics.

**Verification** A daemon-level test that deletes the mgmt-key file mid-run and asserts it reappears with
the same value and that the log line fires; plus a `DoctorCommandTest` update proving the FAIL branch now
only triggers when re-persist is impossible. Gate: `npm run gate`.

### SH-15 — Every refresh rejection collapses to one opaque reason string

**Problem** `RefreshOutcome`'s sealed ladder (L3/G7/G15) correctly separates NoCredentialsFile /

operator revoked access in the provider's dashboard and must re-authorize. A plain **expiry** means re-login.

(`refresh_token_expired` / `refresh_token_reused` / `refresh_token_invalidated` / unknown) to a

the unknown branch (`codex-rs/login/src/auth/manager.rs:1378-1404`). Gemini CLI goes further at the UI layer
with a dedicated `BannedAccountDialog` (appeal URL) separate from `ValidationRequiredError`
(`packages/cli/src/ui/auth/BannedAccountDialog.tsx`). Hermes uses a frozenset of terminal OAuth reasons
(`token_revoked`, `refresh_token_reused`, `unauthorized_client`…) to move a credential to a DEAD state
clearable only by explicit re-login (`agent/credential_pool.py:63-98`).

**splice today** `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/RefreshRetry.kt:49-50`
(`isTerminalRefreshFailure(status, body, json) = status == HTTP_UNAUTHORIZED || status == HTTP_FORBIDDEN ||

(`RefreshAttempt.InvalidGrant("HTTP $status")`);
`gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/auth/RefreshOutcome.kt:26` (`data class Rejected(val reason: String)`

rejection is not an expiry.

**Proposal** Replace `Rejected`'s free-form String with a small sealed `RejectionReason { Expired, Reused,

**Effort** M. **Risk** Medium and the scoping matters: changing `Rejected`'s payload **type** touches all

**Verification** `RefreshOutcomeTest` cases pinning one distinguishable log line per reason; per-provider

### SH-17 — Shim drift is detected on every invocation and never repaired

### SH-18 — No OS-level supervision

**Problem** Recovery from daemon death is entirely reactive and user-initiated: the daemon only comes back
when a wrapper command or `splice restart` / `splice doctor` is next invoked, finds `/health` unreachable,

### SH-20 — Probe cadence, enable/disable, and first-tick jitter are hard-coded

**Problem** `AuthProbeLoop`'s 60s interval is a private companion constant with no Knob entry and no way to

**splice today** `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/AuthProbeLoop.kt:83`

**Proposal** Add two knobs to the existing typed registry — `authProbeIntervalMs` (default 60000, floor ~5s,

verify less often. Keep the first tick immediate (that IS the pre-traffic catch) but apply ±10% jitter to
subsequent intervals, reusing the same `JITTER_LO`/`JITTER_HI` shape `UpstreamClient` and `RefreshRetry`
already share (G4c), so N heads desynchronize within a few cycles.

**Effort** S. **Risk** Low. Additive knobs with documented restart-required semantics (per `Knob.kt`'s own

**Verification** `ConfigServiceTest` cases for the new knobs' defaults, env aliases, and clamp floor; an

### JW-01 — Daemon cold-start stderr goes to `/dev/null`

**Problem** Both cold-start paths launch the JVM with output discarded. If the daemon dies before
`persistentLogger` exists — broken TOML, unwritable state dir, bad `SPLICE_JVM_OPTS`, port stolen, lock
failure — the stack trace is destroyed and the operator sees only
`splice: daemon failed version handshake (expected 0.1.1, got <none>)`. There is no file to tail, no
doctor row, no way forward except knowing to hand-run `java -jar ~/.local/share/splice/splice.jar daemon`.
The codebase already names this hazard — `Daemon.kt:286-288` says a failing head "must NOT abort the whole
daemon with a stack trace to /dev/null" — but the mitigation was per-head isolation, so by construction
everything *upstream* of the head loop still vanishes. **A first-run failure is currently unreadable by
design.**

**Evidence** `BerriAI/litellm#33650` — the Docker image was broken for every fresh deployment after a
version bump because DB migrations failed on a missing `libatomic.so.1` with "no clear error surfaced to the
operator"; the fix was surfacing the cause, not changing the dependency. Docker Desktop's
`docker desktop diagnose` and its documented per-platform log locations exist so a first-run failure is
always readable.

**splice today** The boot order is the load-bearing fact:
`gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/Main.kt:48` runs
`TopologyLoader.loadOrMaterialize(topologyPath)` (which throws on malformed TOML at
`TopologyLoader.kt:61`) and `:50` only then constructs `val log = persistentLogger(statePaths.logsDir)`.
Both cold-start paths discard: `bin/splice-launch:136`
(`nohup java $SPLICE_JVM_OPTS -jar "$JAR" daemon >/dev/null 2>&1 &`) and
`gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/cli/AdminSupport.kt:125` (same string inside `sh -c`) plus
`Redirect.DISCARD` on both streams at `:127-128`. The handshake failure at `bin/splice-launch:144-147`

### JW-04 — An edited `splice.toml` is silently inert

**Problem** Topology is read once at daemon start and is explicitly not hot-reloadable. The launch shim only
replaces a running daemon on a `GATEWAY_VERSION` mismatch, so after an operator edits splice.toml (adds a
head, changes a port, changes `pinned_model`) and relaunches `claudex`, the still-running old daemon serves
the old topology with **zero signal**. Nothing compares the file on disk against what the daemon booted with
— not the shim, not doctor, not the dashboard. This is the classic "I changed the config and nothing
happened" dead end, and the only recovery is knowing that `splice restart` exists and is required.

**Evidence** `router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI` `internal/watcher/diff` computes an exact delta between the running
config/auth set and the file on disk (model hashes, oauth aliases, excluded-models, openai-compat entries) so
a file edit either applies or is visibly reported — hot-diffing is treated as table stakes for a
config-file-driven proxy. `CLIProxyAPI#4433` is the failure mode when it is absent: a stale process against a
rewritten config produced 401s that "look like a proxy bug" and were closed only after the maintainer proved
which config path the process had actually loaded.

**splice today** `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/TopologyLoader.kt:3-4` ("Loaded ONCE at daemon
start … (no hot topology)") and `:56-62`. `bin/splice-launch:104` replaces the daemon only on

`gateway/control/src/main/kotlin/splice/control/ControlServer.kt:392-404` (`controlHealthJson`) carries

**Effort** M. **Risk** Digesting on the shim's hot path adds a file read + hash per launch (negligible

### NF-07 — `HTTP_PROXY`/`HTTPS_PROXY`/`NO_PROXY` are silently ignored for upstream calls

**Problem** Ktor's Java engine only calls `HttpClient.Builder.proxy()` when a Proxy is configured; splice

cannot diagnose it either, because it has no upstream reachability check (NF-23).

**Evidence** `MoonshotAI/kimi-cli#1234` is this bug verbatim — "Environment variable based proxy is not

`trust_env=False`, standard proxy env vars are silently ignored, `kimi login` hangs then times out, and two
duplicate community PRs sat unmerged. aider threads `requestOptions` (proxy, custom CA, custom headers)
through every provider call for exactly this reason, and Continue does the same. CLIProxyAPI builds its
clients through `NewProxyAwareHTTPClient` as a first-class concern.

**splice today** `gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/UpstreamClient.kt:626-656`
(`defaultClient`) configures only `HttpTimeout` (connect 10s / request `totalTimeoutMs` / socket

refresh/login POST. A repo-wide case-insensitive grep for HTTP_PROXY/HTTPS_PROXY/NO_PROXY/proxySelector/
proxyHost/ProxyBuilder across `gateway/`, `bin/`, `install.sh`, `webui/` returns **only**

**Proposal** Add a small `splice.spi.ProxyConfig.fromEnv(envReader)` reading `HTTPS_PROXY`/`https_proxy`,

### NF-13 — Every timeout and retry knob is restart-required

**Problem** `maxInflight` and `maxQueued` are genuinely hot (read per admission through a live lambda). Every

diagnosis: the moment an operator most needs to widen a first-byte timeout or narrow a retry budget is
mid-incident, when a restart is the most expensive action available. `Knob.kt`'s own header is an honest
self-audit of this.

**Evidence** qwen-code exposes timeout/maxRetries/retryInitialDelayMs/retryMaxDelayMs as per-model

computes an exact config delta and applies it incrementally without a process restart. LiteLLM supports
model/provider hot-reloading. opencode exposes `provider.options.timeout` and `chunkTimeout` per provider at
runtime.

**splice today** `gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/config/Knob.kt:9-14` is the explicit self-audit
("nearly every knob is SNAPSHOTTED at Daemon.start … The genuinely hot knobs are maxInflight and maxQueued …

**Proposal** Apply the existing live-lambda pattern to the resilience knobs: change `UpstreamClient`'s

### NF-19 — Queued turns wait forever

**Problem** The `InflightGate` is FIFO with a bounded queue **length** but an unbounded queue **wait**. With
a correctly-small per-account `maxInflight` (NF-02) and long turns (the shipped kimi override allows
40-minute turns), a request admitted to position 20 can wait tens of minutes. Nothing tells the client it is
queued, nothing bounds the wait, and the client-liveness pinger does not start until **after** slot
acquisition — so a queued request is only reaped when Ktor cancels the call, which happens on TCP disconnect
but not on a client that has silently given up at the application layer. The turn eventually runs and burns
tokens for an answer nobody is waiting for.

**Evidence** HAProxy pairs `maxconn` queueing with an explicit
[`timeout queue`](https://docs.haproxy.org/2.8/configuration.html#4.2-timeout%20queue) for exactly this.
Envoy exposes `max_pending_requests` and treats pending overflow as a shed rather than an unbounded wait.
Portkey's `retryHandler` decrements a shared 60s budget as it honours waits and aborts rather than exceeding
the ceiling. CLIProxyAPI ships `max-retry-interval` as "max seconds to wait on a cooling credential before
giving up" (`config.example.yaml:144-152`).

**splice today** `gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/InflightGate.kt:49-60` (`acquire()`) →
`:69` (`private suspend fun awaitTurn()`) suspends via `suspendCancellableCoroutine` with **no timeout
anywhere in the file**; the only bound is `hasQueueCapacityLocked()` at `:67`, which caps queue length (G21)
and never queue wait. The pinger claim holds too:
`gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/head/HeadServer.kt:265` calls `acquireSlotOrRespond(call)`
before `materializeOrRespond`/`prepareTurn`, and the client-liveness pinger is launched at
`TurnDriver.kt:377` inside `driveOneTurn` — so a queued waiter has no liveness detection at all.

**Proposal** Add a `maxQueueWaitMs` knob (default ~60_000, `0 = unlimited` to match the
maxInflight/maxQueued sentinel convention) and wrap the `awaitTurn()` suspension in `withTimeoutOrNull`. On
expiry, free the FIFO spot (the existing `invokeOnCancellation` path already handles this correctly) and
answer with the same 529 + `Retry-After` terminal as NF-08, message "queued longer than Nms — retry". This
turns an invisible stall into a deterministic, honest shed the client can act on.

**Effort** S. **Risk** A legitimately busy head now sheds requests it would previously have eventually
served. The default must be generous relative to real turn times (60s is well under a single kimi turn, but
the queue moves at turn-*completion* rate, not turn-duration rate); ship it observable via NF-10's
`shedRequests` before tightening. Keep `0 = unlimited` so the old behaviour is one TOML line away.

**Verification** New case in `gateway/provider-spi/src/test/kotlin/InflightGateTest.kt` (which already covers
fifo, overflow, hot-resize, cancel-while-queued): a waiter past `maxQueueWaitMs` is rejected, frees its FIFO
spot, and the next waiter is admitted normally. New case in `HeadServerCapacityTest` asserting the 529 shape.
Gate: `npm run gate`.

### CX-14 — 8 MB request-body cap is a hardcoded constant with no knob

**Problem** `maxRequestBytes` defaults to 8 MB and is never wired to a Knob or to TOML; nothing in the knob
registry mentions it. Anthropic's own Messages API accepts substantially larger bodies. A Claude Code session
that has accumulated a few screenshots (base64 images are ~1.37× the raw bytes and the whole transcript rides
on every turn) crosses 8 MB and every subsequent turn 413s. The error is honest — but it is unfixable from
config, it looks like a splice bug, and `splice doctor` has nothing to say about it.

**Evidence** `envoyproxy/ai-gateway#1662`: large legitimate responses failing on an undocumented default
buffer limit, with users having to independently discover a **second** undocumented limit after fixing the
first. `portkey-ai/gateway#1127`: a non-configurable transport default (300s headers timeout) causing hard
failures with no way to raise the ceiling.

**splice today** `gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/head/HeadServer.kt:80`
(`val maxRequestBytes: Int = DEFAULT_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES`), `:91`
(`public const val DEFAULT_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES: Int = 8 * 1024 * 1024`), enforced via `receiveBodyBounded` at
`:353` and `:380` with `CONTENT_TOO_LARGE_STATUS = 413` at `:466`. `Knob.kt`'s full enum has no
`maxRequestBytes` entry, so the cap is unreachable from TOML or env.

**Proposal** Add a `maxRequestBytes` NUMBER knob (default 8 MiB, alias `CLAUDEX_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES`,
restart-required like its siblings) with a normalization floor, thread it through `HeadDeps` at Daemon
assembly, and make the 413 body name the current limit **and the knob to raise it** — the same
fix-in-the-error idiom `splice doctor` uses everywhere else.

**Effort** S. **Risk** Raising the cap raises the per-turn heap held by the materialization gate; keep the
default at 8 MiB and let the operator opt in, noting the interaction with `RequestMaterializationGate`'s
permit count in the knob's doc comment.

**Verification** `ConfigServiceTest` gains a normalization case for the new knob;
`HeadServerIntegrationTest` (which already constructs with `maxRequestBytes = 1_024` at `:109` and asserts
413 at `:166`) asserts the 413 body names the knob, plus a raised-knob case that accepts a body above the
default. Gate: `npm run gate`.

### SH-19 — Topology and per-head knobs are read exactly once at boot

**Problem** "Adding a backend is a TOML edit" is true of the edit and false of the activation:
`TopologyLoader.loadOrMaterialize` runs once in `runDaemon()` and the resulting `Topology` is passed to the
`Daemon` constructor. There is no `WatchService`, no reload endpoint, no SIGHUP handler. `Knob.kt`'s header
states the situation plainly — nearly every knob is snapshotted at `Daemon.start`, and only
maxInflight/maxQueued are genuinely live. So changing a head's port, model, reasoning effort, dialect,
per-head override, or adding a head at all requires `splice restart`, which drops in-flight turns on every
other head. For the headline configuration story that is a sharp edge, and for a long-lived daemon it means
the operator's mental model ("I edited the TOML") and reality ("the daemon is running this morning's TOML")
routinely diverge with nothing reporting the drift.

**Evidence** CLIProxyAPI computes an exact delta between running config/auth state and the file on disk
(model hashes, oauth aliases, excluded-models, openai-compat entries) and applies only the delta
(`internal/watcher/watcher.go`, `internal/watcher/diff/*.go`). LiteLLM advertises model/provider hot-reloading
as a first-class proxy feature. nginx/HAProxy reload-without-drop is the general precedent.

**splice today** `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/Main.kt:47-48` is the single
`TopologyLoader.configPath()` / `loadOrMaterialize(topologyPath)` call, and a repo-wide grep of
`gateway/**.kt` for `WatchService`, `SIGHUP`, and `reload` returns **zero** hits — there is no reload path of
any kind. `Knob.kt:9-14` self-audits the snapshot problem; `:143-177` confirms the `restartRequired` spread.
`RestartCommand.kt:9-29` is the only answer. Note also that `AGENTS.md:77-79` documents the **legacy Node**
control plane as hot-applying everything but four keys — which is now false for the Kotlin daemon and is
user-visible doc drift worth fixing alongside.

**Proposal** Two tiers, smallest first. **(1) Drift detection** (cheap, high value on its own): stat the
topology file on the probe cadence; when mtime/size changes, re-parse in-process, diff against the running
`Topology`, and log + surface on `/health` and the dashboard exactly which heads changed and that a restart
is needed. That alone converts a silent divergence into a visible one, and it is the same digest JW-04
computes. **(2) Delta apply** (larger): for the subset genuinely safe to apply live — a new head (start it),
a removed head (drain and stop it), and `[heads.<key>.overrides]` changes (`ConfigService` already layers
these) — apply the delta and log it; leave port/dialect/provider changes flagged as restart-required rather
than pretending. Expose as `POST /api/config/reload` (bearer-guarded like every other mutating route) plus a
`splice reload` verb.

**Effort** L. **Risk** Medium-high for tier 2: starting and stopping heads at runtime touches the exact
lifecycle code the BS-4 shutdown incidents came from, and a partially-applied delta is worse than no reload.
Tier 1 is near-zero risk and should land first and independently. **Sequence JW-06 before tier 2** — with no
per-head field on `ConfigLayers` the dashboard cannot show which head an override applies to, so a reload
would deepen an observability gap it cannot explain.

**Verification** Tier 1: a Daemon-level test that mutates the topology file mid-run and asserts `/health`
reports config drift naming the changed heads. Tier 2: `DaemonTest` cases for add-head-live and
remove-head-live, plus an assertion that a port change is reported restart-required rather than silently
applied. Gate: `npm run gate`.

### SH-21 — The give-up error names no healthy sibling head (G27's own "cheap version")

*(Absorbs NF-17, which proposed the same seam for the RATE_LIMIT branch. One `siblingHints` thread covers
both classifications; shipping them separately would produce two conflicting Provider signature changes.)*

**Problem** When a head gives up — auth dead, quota exhausted, sustained upstream failure — the terminal
error frame tells the user what went wrong on **that** head and, for auth, which login command to run. It
does not tell them the one thing that would unblock them in ten seconds: which other configured head is
currently healthy and would serve this session. The operator's actual recovery is to remember their own
topology, quit, and relaunch under a different wrapper — information the daemon holds precisely and
completely. For the quota case the daemon even humanizes the reset as "6d 4h" and still says nothing about
what to do.

**Evidence** This is **not** a proposal to build cross-head failover — that is G27's wontfix and the field
evidence supports it (`envoyproxy/ai-gateway#1866` reports its priority+retry failover not triggering as
documented even when built on mature primitives; `anomalyco/opencode#26292` shows competing unmerged
implementations after 97 reactions of demand). It is the alternative G27's **own text** names:
"cross-head failover (session state can't migrate; cheap version = name healthy sibling heads in the give-up
error)" (`dev/research/gateway-best-practices-2026-07-18.md:84`) — never built. Gemini CLI's practice of
enriching the thrown error object itself with the literal remediation command, so the fix travels with the
error regardless of how it is later displayed, is the mechanism (`enrichQuotaError`,
`packages/core/src/utils/retry.ts:215-249`). gemini-cli's `BannedAccountDialog` is the same idea at the UI
layer, and `google-gemini/gemini-cli#24937` (95 reactions, 131 comments, maintainer-created) is a tracking
issue for exactly the "opaque quota dead end" experience.

**splice today** `gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/head/TurnDriver.kt:326-327` appends only the
head's **own** `provider.loginCommand`; `:316` and `:567` use only `provider.loginHint()` (defined at `:666`);
a grep for sibling/siblingHints across `gateway/` main sources returns **zero** hits — no head ever names
another. The quota facts for the RATE_LIMIT branch are already extracted at
`gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/UpstreamFailureClassifier.kt:100-108` (`quotaSuffix` →
plan_type + humanized reset). The daemon-wide topology exists at `Daemon.kt` (the head map, per-head
`loginCommand` threading at `:364/:425/:492/:538/:584/:619`) and is already aggregated for `/health` in
`ControlServer.kt`.

**Proposal** Thread a `siblingHints: () -> List<String>` (healthy head wrapper commands, excluding self)
from the Daemon into each head's error path, and append one clause to terminal **AUTHENTICATION** and
**quota-exhausted/RATE_LIMIT** messages: `other heads available now: claude-grok, claudeor`. Compute it from
SH-03's health state so it never advertises a head that is itself dead — a wrong suggestion is worse than
none. Mirror the same line in `splice doctor` and on the dashboard head plate. Strictly message enrichment:
no routing, no session migration, no retry against another head. Explicitly **not** a reopening of G27.

**Effort** S. **Risk** Low, with one caveat worth naming: advertising a sibling makes the failover UX visible
and will likely generate "why doesn't it just switch automatically" pressure. The honest answer (session
state cannot migrate mid-conversation) belongs in the message or the docs, not left implicit. Phrase the
hint as "configured and credentialed", not "guaranteed working" (NF-23's reachability probe is what would
make it stronger). Adds one field to the Provider surface, which several provider modules construct.

**Verification** `HeadServerIntegrationTest` case (which already pins the G19 login-hint append): a head
whose auth is dead, with one healthy sibling configured, emits an error frame containing both its own login
hint and the sibling's wrapper command; a 429 with a quota body produces the same clause on the RATE_LIMIT
branch; a single-head topology omits the clause entirely with no trailing punctuation artifact. Gate:
`npm run gate`.

### JW-22 — No update path or update awareness

**Problem** There is no `splice update` verb and nothing ever tells an operator that a newer release exists.
Upgrading means remembering that install.sh is the mechanism and re-fetching it by hand. For a tool whose
whole failure story is "a Claude Code update can break splice at any time" (the README's own words), the
operator most in need of an upgrade is the one whose install just broke — and the broken install is silent
about the fix having already shipped. The version machinery to build this on already exists and is exercised:
`GATEWAY_VERSION`, `SHIM_VERSION`, the `/health` handshake, and the shim's automatic stale-daemon
self-replacement.

**Evidence** rustup exposes three explicit auto-update modes (enable / disable / check-only) plus CI
detection via `RUSTUP_CI` so update behaviour is a deliberate operator choice rather than a default. Neither

**splice today** `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/cli/Command.kt:44-55` — the verb table has no
update/upgrade. Versions exist at `gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/Versions.kt:6,11` and are already

`DoctorCommand.kt:128-139` CLI-vs-daemon). `install.sh:300-357` already implements staged atomic replacement
with a `restore_previous_artifacts` rollback.

**Proposal** Two separable pieces, in order. **(a) `splice update`**: fetch and verify the release exactly as
install.sh does — reusing its sha256 + Sigstore attestation gates — then `splice restart`. **Note the
correction**: the existing `splice install` verb (`InstallCommand.kt:35-38`) only re-links wrappers and the
shim from an already-present jar and hard-checks
`launch shim not found at $launchShim (run install.sh)`, so there is **no in-CLI fetch/verify path today** —
this is genuinely new code and must reuse install.sh's gates rather than reinvent them. **(b)** An opt-in,
**default-OFF** `updateCheck` knob: when enabled, **doctor and only doctor** (never the daemon, never the
shim's hot path) queries the releases endpoint at most once per day, caches the answer under the state dir,
and prints an INFO row when a newer version exists.

**Effort** M. **Risk** Any background network call from a loopback-only tool is a posture change (it does not
violate the loopback lock, which is about inbound exposure, but it is a change) and must therefore be
default-off, doctor-scoped, cached, and bounded. Auto-installing is explicitly out of scope: `splice update`
stays operator-invoked. This is the lowest-priority item in this document precisely because it adds surface
rather than removing failure.

**Verification** New `gateway/app/src/test/kotlin/UpdateCommandTest.kt` with a fake installer script on PATH
asserting the invoke-then-restart ordering and a non-zero exit when the installer fails. For (b): assert the
check is skipped entirely when the knob is off (no socket opened), and that a cached answer is reused within

## 8. Improvements — correctness

### CX-19 — There is no response-side golden

**Problem** Every dialect has a **request**-byte golden that fails on drift in any field, and the e2e emits a
receipt binding it to a live 200. The **response** direction — the exact Anthropic SSE bytes splice writes to
Claude Code — has no equivalent end-to-end pin. Event ordering across a multi-round turn, block-index
allocation, mirror-block placement, signature synthesis, and stop_reason derivation from a real upstream
terminal are asserted only by whatever component-level tests happen to cover them. That means a refactor can
silently reorder or drop a frame class with the suite green, and — more consequentially — **every other item
in §8 lands without a regression net for the wire shape it changes**. Build this first.

**Evidence** `BerriAI/litellm#25214`/`#25216`/`#27492`: the first chunk after a content-block-type transition
(reasoning → text) silently dropped on reasoning models, "a blocker for Claude Code usability"; `#2391`/
`#1894`/`#2162`: SSE truncated across chunk boundaries and mode switches.
`google-gemini/gemini-cli#26956`: "function call turn comes immediately after a user turn" — transcript
construction violating the strict turn-shape contract. These are precisely the class a byte-diff of the
emitted stream catches and scenario tests do not.

**splice today** Request goldens exist and are explicit about it:
`gateway/dialect-openai-responses/src/test/kotlin/ResponsesContractTest.kt:59,77,94`,
`gateway/dialect-openai-chat/src/test/kotlin/ChatContractTest.kt:29`,
`gateway/dialect-anthropic-passthrough/src/test/kotlin/PassthroughContractTest.kt:27` — every
`assertGoldenContract` call site asserts a **request** `JsonObject` ("the golden is the EXACT upstream
request"). Grep finds no golden fixture files or byte-diff of emitted SSE anywhere; `checks/e2e/stream_probe.py`
validates latency budgets and the contract client-side on live traffic only. **Scope correction:** the
response direction is not unasserted — `SseEmitterTest` is a dedicated 13-case suite capturing real frame
text via `write = { frames.add(it) }` (`SseEmitterTest.kt:19-32`) and already pins the lazy
message_start/ping/block-start ordering (`:36`), block-index allocation (`:39-45`), stop_reason precedence
(`:64`), signature-delta placement between thinking deltas and block stop (`:132`), terminal idempotence
(`:77`), the single-error-then-seal path (`:88`), and abandon (`:99`). The genuine residual is narrower and
should be stated as such: **no end-to-end fixture-through-translator-through-emitter byte golden**, so
cross-component drift is unpinned.

**Proposal** Add a response-side golden per dialect: feed a canned upstream SSE fixture (text + reasoning
summary + a parallel tool call + terminal) through the **real** translator into a **real** `SseEmitter`
writing to a `StringBuilder`, and byte-diff the whole emitted stream against a checked-in golden. Reuse
`assertGoldenContract`'s regenerate-deliberately discipline. Three fixtures per dialect (clean turn, tool
turn, failure terminal) is enough to lock ordering, indices, signature synthesis, mirror placement, and
stop_reason.

**Effort** M. **Risk** Goldens that are too eager become churn — scope them to three canonical fixtures per
dialect and keep the message id and timestamps deterministic (`SseEmitter.create` already accepts an
injectable messageId). Regeneration must stay a deliberate delete-review-rerun, exactly as the request
goldens document.

**Verification** The goldens **are** the gate: `:dialect-*:test` plus `:gateway:test` under `npm run gate`.
Landing check: deliberately reorder one frame in `SseEmitter` and confirm the golden goes red — a red-green
proof **before** the fixtures are trusted.

### CX-01 — Tool-call arguments are never validated as JSON before a turn is Success

**Problem** Both OpenAI-family translators stream whatever argument text the backend sends straight into
`input_json_delta` and then close the block. Nothing ever parses the accumulated buffer. A backend that
truncates arguments mid-string but still emits a terminal (`response.completed` / `finish_reason:tool_calls`)
produces a Success carrying a well-formed-looking `tool_use` whose `input` is corrupt JSON. Claude Code then
either hard-errors on parse or dispatches the tool with garbage. The same site also ships `input: {}` when a
tool block opens and no arguments ever arrive — the Responses dialect opens tool blocks **eagerly** on
`output_item.added`, so the block exists before any args do.

**Evidence** `anomalyco/opencode#36766` ("fix(llm): handle truncated OpenAI tool arguments", open,
2026-07-13): the OpenAI Responses path "intermittently finalizes tool calls with truncated JSON arguments
(patch call argument text ending mid-string)" and treats it as terminal, killing the turn. `#38747`/`#34652`
are the same class via double-JSON-encoded args. `BerriAI/litellm#32607`: per-chunk id churn broke downstream
tool-call-argument aggregation. `router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI#4575`/`#3551`: tool-schema/arg drift across dialect
boundaries.

**splice today** `gateway/dialect-openai-chat/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/chat/ChatStreamTranslator.kt:265-285`
(`applyToolCall` appends to `pending.args` and calls `sink.inputJsonDelta(opened, args)` with no validation)
and `:289-296` (`openPendingTool` flushes `pending.args.toString()` the same way);
`gateway/dialect-openai-responses/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/responses/ResponsesStreamTranslator.kt:539-559`
(`onArgs` streams deltas and on `.done` harvests `arguments` and calls `sink.closeBlock(b.index)` — never a
parse). The `input: {}` half is real too: the `.done` branch only fills when `!b.sawDelta && full.isNotEmpty()`,
so an opened tool block with zero args closes empty. Repo-wide, `Json.parseToJsonElement` appears in dialect
main sources at exactly three sites — `ReasoningCache.kt:128`, `ToolSurfaceRecovery.kt:37`,
`ResponsesStreamTranslator.kt:686` (tool_search args) — **none** of which is the function-call argument
buffer.

**Proposal** Accumulate per-tool argument text alongside the wire writes (a `StringBuilder` per open tool
block, capped by NF-06's runaway guard). At block close (`onArgs` `.done` / `closeOpenBlocks` in Responses,
`openPendingTool` + `flushPendingTools` in Chat) run `Json.parseToJsonElement(args)`; on
`SerializationException` or on an empty buffer for an *opened* tool, latch a translator-level
`toolArgsInvalid` reason. Route it exactly like `ChatStreamTranslator`'s existing `runawayGuard` latch
(`:52-56,111-126`): `terminalOutcome` returns
`TurnOutcome.Failure(API_ERROR, "<head>: backend returned malformed tool arguments for <name> — retry",
providerReported = true)` instead of Success. Add a `PerfKeys` counter `tool_args_invalid` so the class is
greppable in the perf JSONL.

**Effort** M. **Risk** A backend that legitimately streams non-JSON freeform tool arguments (OpenAI
`custom_tool_call`) would be failed — key the check on the `function_call`/`tool_calls` item types only,
which are the only ones splice opens tool blocks for today. Second: turning a previously-Success turn into a
Failure changes retry volume — bound it by the existing `UpstreamClient` retry budget, no new retry loop.

**Verification** New cases in `ResponsesStreamTranslatorTest.kt` and `ChatStreamTranslatorTest.kt`: (a) args
truncated mid-string + `response.completed` → Failure not Success; (b) tool item opened, zero arg deltas,
`item.done` → Failure; (c) valid args unchanged (byte-identical wire). Gate: `npm run gate`.

### CX-02 — Compaction instructions exist ONLY in the Responses dialect

**splice today** `gateway/dialect-openai-responses/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/responses/ResponsesRequestBuilder.kt:371-383`

non-retryable-looking after the first attempt: keep the honest error but drop the " — retry" suffix when the

**Effort** S. **Risk** Passthrough's whole design is verbatim forwarding (`copyUnhandledFields` at `:55`) —

**Verification** `ChatRequestBuilderTest.kt` + `PassthroughRequestBuilderTest.kt`: a compact build contains

### JW-07 — No Claude Code version-skew canary

**Problem** Compaction detection is positive-marker-only against five verbatim sentences traced out of Claude
Code **2.1.207**. When Claude Code rewords its summarizer prompt, detection silently stops firing:

and nobody looks. doctor captures `claude --version` output but only prints it; it never compares against the
version the markers were traced from, and no constant anywhere records that version.

**Evidence** `1rgs/claude-code-proxy#12` and `#35` — "Doesn't seem to work with the latest Claude Code
0.2.69": a proxy written against an older Messages API shape breaking silently as the CLI moved, with no
compatibility contract or version pin between them; both issues remain **open**.
`envoyproxy/ai-gateway#2346`/`#2091` show the disciplined counterpart — a startup compatibility check between
binary and config schema, whose absence produced hard post-upgrade failures.

**splice today** `gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/compact/Compact.kt:28-38` — the doc comment
literally reads "Every verbatim summarizer instruction Claude Code 2.1.207 emits (binary-traced). On drift:
add the new verbatim sentence here + a fixture", over five hardcoded sentences. The instrument is passive:
`ShadowClassifier.record()` (`:109-127`) logs one `[shadow-compact] compact=… has_marker=…` line per request
into a 500-row ring and rings nothing (see CX-12 — `tail()` has zero callers). Doctor captures but never
compares: `gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/cli/DoctorProbes.kt:26-36` declares
`BinarySpec("claude", listOf(FLAG_VERSION), …)` and `:89` folds the output into `capturedVersion(...)` as a
display string. `gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/Versions.kt` holds `GATEWAY_VERSION` and
`SHIM_VERSION` only — no Claude Code version constant exists anywhere.

**Proposal** (1) Add `CLAUDE_CODE_MARKERS_TRACED_AT = "2.1.207"` and `CLAUDE_CODE_TESTED_MAX` to
`splice.core.Versions`. (2) doctor's prerequisites section parses the `claude --version` output and emits a
WARN when the installed version exceeds `CLAUDE_CODE_TESTED_MAX`: "compaction markers were traced against
2.1.207 — if /compact stops working, re-trace", with the fix pointing at the CHANGELOG note. (3) Daemon-side
canary: after N (say 200) classified requests with **zero** `compact=true`, log a single
`[daemon] compaction canary: 200 requests, 0 compact classifications — markers may be stale` and expose the
ratio on `/api/compact` so the dashboard's compact feed shows it. Reset the counter on each daemon start.

**Effort** M. **Risk** The "zero compactions in N requests" heuristic false-fires for a short-session user
who genuinely never compacts, so it must be a **once-per-daemon INFO/WARN**, never a FAIL and never a

### CX-03 — `count_tokens` estimate is body-bytes/3

**Problem** `handleCountTokens` estimates tokens as `ceil(total request bytes / 3)` over the **whole JSON
body**. A single 1 MB base64 screenshot contributes ~350,000 estimated tokens against a real image cost of
roughly 1–2k. Claude Code uses `count_tokens` for pre-flight context sizing; a turn with two screenshots
reports a context that looks larger than any window, so the client compacts immediately (or refuses the turn)
on a session that is actually nearly empty. The failure is invisible: the endpoint returns 200 with a
plausible integer.

**Evidence** `MoonshotAI/kimi-cli#2011`: a client-side context-usage indicator showing <50% while the
provider rejects the same request for exceeding the limit — client token accounting diverging from the real
payload is a recurring, user-visible class. `Aider-AI/aider#3618`/`#4583` ("model database ain't right") is
the same divergence from the other direction. `router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI#4195`/`#3744` catalog/window drift.

**splice today** `gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/head/HeadServer.kt:392-397` —
`((body.bytes + TOKEN_ESTIMATE_BYTES - 1) / TOKEN_ESTIMATE_BYTES).coerceAtLeast(1)` with

**Effort** S. **Risk** **Asymmetric — undercounting is worse than overcounting** for autocompact timing.

**Verification** `gateway/gateway/src/test/kotlin/head/HeadServerIntegrationTest.kt` already has

### SH-10 — The kimi head rewrites its credential file from scratch on every refresh

**Problem** `GrokAuthProvider` and `CodexAuthProvider` both **merge** rotated tokens onto the on-disk object

beside ours (the 2026-07-18 audit finding, documented in grok's own `mergedAuthJson` comment).

vector, not a splice-internal detail.

**Evidence** `google-gemini/gemini-cli#21691` is the canonical instance of this exact failure (a refresh write
that dropped fields left sessions dead ~1h later); the upstream fix is a merge that preserves refresh_token
when the response omits one (`packages/core/src/mcp/oauth-token-storage.ts:146-153`). kimi-cli itself performs
a keyring→file **migration** on load (`oauth.py:421-437`), i.e. the file's field set is not frozen and has
already changed once.

**splice today** `gateway/provider-kimi/src/main/kotlin/splice/provider/kimi/KimiAuthProvider.kt:159`

**Effort** S. **Risk** Low. Strictly additive to what is preserved on disk; the only behavioural change is

### SH-11 — `KeyStore` write/unset destroys every other key when the file is momentarily unreadable

**Problem** `KeyStore.write()` and `unset()` are read-modify-write over `entries()`, and `entries()` collapses
**any** read failure to an empty map via `getOrDefault(emptyMap())`. So a transient read error — an EINTR, a
partially-written file from a concurrent writer, a permissions blip — makes `write()` believe the store is
empty and persist a file containing **only the key being written**, silently deleting every other API key the
operator had stored. `persist()` rebuilds the whole file from the map, so the loss is total, not partial.
Even without a read failure, two concurrent `splice key set` invocations (a human plus the login-interception
token-capture hook, which is exactly the automation splice ships) each read the same snapshot and the second
write wins. auth.json got `CredentialLock` specifically because this race killed a token once (G1); keys.toml
is the same class of durable credential state with none of that protection.

**Evidence** `google-gemini/gemini-cli#26715`/`#28270`: corrupted local credential/config files causing
crash-loops and data loss rather than graceful degradation, with the upstream conclusion that a config read
failure must never be treated as "empty". opencode's cross-process flock with staleness detection and
heartbeats guards exactly this shared-state class (`packages/core/src/util/flock.ts`). kimi-cli PR
`#1996`'s central lesson is the same one in the credential domain: on an ambiguous failure, never delete —
suppress.

**splice today** `gateway/core/src/main/kotlin/splice/core/config/KeyStore.kt` — `entries()` ends in
`.getOrDefault(emptyMap())`; `write()` (`:48-61`) and `unset()` (`:63-71`) both do
`entries().toMutableMap()` → `persist()` (`:60`). Zero FileLock/Mutex/synchronized in the class, while the
auth.json equivalent got exactly that as G1 (`done e970691`) —
`gateway/provider-spi/src/main/kotlin/splice/spi/CredentialLock.kt:25`.

**Proposal** Two changes. (1) **Split the read paths**: keep the tolerant `getOrDefault(emptyMap())` for
`read()`/`names()` (display paths), but give `write()`/`unset()` a strict variant returning `Result` that
**aborts the write** on a read failure with a loud message ("keys.toml unreadable — refusing to write,
existing keys preserved"). Distinguishing "file absent" (legitimately empty, safe to write) from "file
unreadable" (unknown, unsafe) is the whole fix. (2) Route `write()`/`unset()` through a file lock.
**Module-direction caveat**: `KeyStore` lives in `:core` and `CredentialLock` in `:provider-spi`, and
`:provider-spi` depends on `:core`, not the reverse — so the primitive must **move down into `:core`** (or a
lock helper be added there). This is not a drop-in reuse.

**Effort** S. **Risk** Low. (1) can turn a previously-silent success into a visible failure, which is the
point. (2) makes a CLI-rate operation take an advisory lock; with SH-06's bounded wait it cannot hang.

**Verification** New cases in `gateway/core/src/test/kotlin/KeyStoreTest.kt`: an unreadable keys.toml
(permissions stripped) → `write()` fails loudly and the on-disk content is **byte-identical afterwards**
(today it is replaced by a one-key file); two concurrent writes of different names → both present. Gate:
`npm run gate`.

**Evidence** `router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI#3624`/`#3663`: replaying reasoning artifacts back to a backend in the
wrong shape produces 400s and corrupted multi-turn state; `#4429` same for message framing around reasoning.
`MoonshotAI/kimi-cli#2496`: an internal compaction marker leaking into the visible transcript because the
writer and the re-reader disagreed about what the text was. splice's own `AGENTS.md` L1 records the
**measured** finding that replaying prior opaque reasoning makes fresh reasoning thinner — the same hazard,
different channel. **The behavioural harm claimed here is unmeasured speculation, unlike that L1 finding, and
must be treated as a hypothesis with a measurement gate — never as a proven defect.**

**splice today** `gateway/gateway/src/main/kotlin/splice/gateway/reasoning/Mirror.kt:16` defines the external
wire contract (`"\n[reasoning summary]\n${thinking.trim()}\n"`) and `:33` writes it via `sink.addTextBlock`.
On the way back upstream it is an ordinary `TextBlock` forwarded verbatim at
`gateway/dialect-openai-responses/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/responses/ResponsesRequestBuilder.kt:520`
(`is TextBlock -> sink.add(roleText(role, block.text))`) and
`gateway/dialect-openai-chat/src/main/kotlin/splice/dialect/chat/ChatRequestBuilder.kt:145`. A repo-wide grep
for the marker string shows nothing strips or relabels it on any request path — the only non-test hits are
`Mirror.kt` itself, `Knob.kt:83`, and unrelated comments.

**Proposal** Do **not** remove the mirror (it is the headline feature and it must survive Claude Code's
compaction, which reads the transcript). Instead relabel it on the way back upstream: when a leading assistant
`TextBlock` matches the mirror contract prefix, forward it wrapped as an explicit recap
("[prior turn reasoning recap, not shown to the user]") rather than as plain assistant answer text — a
one-site change in each request builder driven by one shared predicate in `Mirror.kt`. Keep compact turns
forwarding it verbatim (the summarizer must see it). **Measure before defaulting on**: land it behind a knob
and compare turn quality on the codex head.

**Effort** M. **Risk** **Highest blast radius in this document.** It touches the headline feature's wire
contract, which the file header explicitly says external transcript tooling keys on. The transcript bytes
Claude Code stores must **NOT** change; only the upstream request representation may. **If the measurement
shows no improvement, ship nothing** — that clause is load-bearing, not a formality.

**Verification** A shared `isMirrorBlock` predicate test in `:core`; `ResponsesRequestBuilderTest` /
`ChatRequestBuilderTest` cases asserting the relabel fires only for mirror-shaped leading assistant text and
never for user text; `ReplayMirrorTest` must stay green byte-for-byte (the transcript side is untouched). Then
a live A/B via `checks/e2e/heads-e2e.sh --tier 2` on the codex head before flipping the default. Gate:
`npm run gate`.

## 9. Reconsiderations — decisions this brief puts back on the table

One candidate survived verification as a **decision surface** rather than a proposal. It is presented with
its counter-evidence intact; the call is the operator's.

Of G27's four non-goals, three found **no** new evidence in this sweep and stay closed without further
discussion: *stream resumption cursor* (Claude Code still sends no `Last-Event-ID`; qwen-code's `#7457` is
active evidence that a resume cursor scoped to the wrong lifetime silently mis-resumes, which validates the
non-goal rather than challenging it); *hedging* (paid non-idempotent completions — gRPC A6's own guidance
restricts hedging to idempotent methods, and splice's post-send double-billing risk at `UpstreamClient.kt:320-324`
is the same objection); *multi-endpoint redispatch* (single URL per head is architectural).

### NF-24 — RECONSIDER: opt-in, default-off, turn-boundary-only fallback head for a hard-down primary

**What was decided and why.** `dev/research/gateway-gaps-tracker.md:62` lists "cross-head mid-session
failover" as `wontfix (by design)`, with the 2026-07-18 rationale that session state cannot migrate and the
cheap version is naming healthy siblings in the give-up error (that cheap version is SH-21, and it is
proposed). Structurally there is nothing to reuse: each head is its own `HeadServer` on its own port with its
own `Provider`, `UpstreamClient`, `ModelCatalog`, and `UsageStore` (`gateway/app/src/main/kotlin/splice/app/Daemon.kt:669-700`),
and the client is bound to one port by the wrapper's `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL`. A fallback would have to be a NEW
head-to-head dispatch inside `HeadServer`, not a config toggle.

**What changed since 2026-07-18.** Three independent competitors now ship or are actively building this, and
the demand signal is the largest in the sweep. Verified live, not from training: `sst/opencode#7602`
("[FEATURE]: Native Model Fallback / Failover Support", **open**, 97 reactions, 29 comments) plus
`anomalyco/opencode#26292` ("feat(opencode): add LLM provider fallback chain", **open**) and a
widely-installed third-party plugin (`opencode-model-fallback-chain`) because demand outpaced merging.
qwen-code **shipped** an ordered `modelFallbacks` chain gated strictly to 429/503/529 with a retryable
diagnosis and a UI editor (`packages/core/src/utils/retryErrorClassification.ts`, `isFallbackEligible`).
CLIProxyAPI ships quota-exceeded auto-downgrade (`quota-exceeded.switch-project`/`switch-preview-model`,
`config.example.yaml:190-194`). Envoy AI Gateway ships priority-tiered `backendRefs`
(`examples/provider_fallback/fallback.yaml`). Separately, the operator's stated north star for this audit is
literally "never fails", and when a head's account is quota-exhausted for days (the classifier already
humanizes resets of 142h) splice's honest answer today is a clean error and a manual wrapper switch — the
session is over.

**What has not changed, and argues for holding the line.** Three things, one of them splice-specific and
decisive. (1) `envoyproxy/ai-gateway#1866` ("Provider Fallback & Token Rate Limiting Not Working in Envoy AI
Gateway", **open**) reports that even Envoy's mature priority+retry failover does not reliably trigger in the
field — failure **classification**, not routing, is the hard part, and splice would be building the
classification too. (2) opencode's version is still unmerged after three competing designs and 97 reactions.
(3) **Splice-specific**: switching heads changes the MODEL mid-conversation, which invalidates the warm
prompt-cache prefix — the exact "compaction ate my subscription" failure the cache law exists to prevent —
breaks reasoning-summary continuity in the mirror, and hands the user a transcript silently authored by two
different models.

**What it would cost, if pursued.** The only defensible shape is: a per-head `[heads.<key>.fallback] head =
"<other-key>"`, **default absent**, firing ONLY when (a) the primary is in a hard cooldown or returned a
quota-exhausted 429 **before any byte of this turn reached the client**, and (b) the turn is not a compaction
turn (compaction must stay on the session's own model — the cache law), re-dispatching the already-materialized
request to the sibling head's `Provider` at the **turn boundary** and prefixing the response with a visible
`[served by <head>]` mirror note so the switch is never silent. Never mid-stream, never mid-round, never for a
fold/re-anchor continuation. Effort **L**. Verification: a new
`gateway/gateway/src/test/kotlin/head/HeadFallbackTest.kt` proving (a) no fallback fires after any content
frame, (b) no fallback on a compact turn, (c) the served-by note is always present, (d) with no
`[heads.*.fallback]` table configured the byte-for-byte behaviour of every existing `HeadServer` test is
unchanged; plus `checks/e2e/heads-e2e.sh` tier 2 across two live heads.

**Framing.** This entry exists so the trade-off is made explicitly rather than by omission. It is a decision
surface, not a build recommendation — the verification that produced it recommends keeping G27 as `wontfix`
and shipping SH-21 instead. Publishing it as a proposal would violate the locked decision it is asking the
operator to consciously re-affirm.

## Appendix A — method and provenance

**Workflow shape.** Five stages, fan-out at each: (1) **blind sweep** — five independent angles over the
public landscape with no shared vocabulary, so overlap between angles is corroboration rather than echo;
(2) **rank and deep-dive** — 12 targets selected for structural proximity to splice, each dossiered for
features *and* mined for the failure modes its users actually report; (3) **inventory** — 7 splice subsystems
read from source by dedicated agents, each producing a have / absent / fragile ledger with `file:line`;
(4) **synthesis** — four thematic generators (never-fails, self-heals, just-works, correctness) crossing the
dossiers against the inventory; (5) **adversarial verification** — four slices, each verifier re-reading the
actual tree and instructed to kill anything already landed, already tracked as done, locked as a non-goal, or
unfounded.

**Counts.** 94 candidate projects swept across 5 angles. 12 deep-dived with issue trackers mined. 7 splice
subsystems inventoried. 85 candidates generated. 85 confirmed, 0 rejected, of which 7 required a corrected
premise. 1 published as a decision surface (§9), 1 merged into its twin, 1 merged as a duplicate site → 82
subsections across §5–§8 covering 84 candidate IDs.

**Models.** Sweep and inventory agents ran on `haiku` (gathering and inventory tiers). Dossier mining and
verification ran on `sonnet` where the spec was pinned and on `opus` where multi-constraint judgment was
required. The four thematic synthesizers and all four verification slices ran on `opus`. No agent inherited a
parent model.

**What was searched.** GitHub issue trackers (open and recently-closed, sampled per project: opencode 55 of
